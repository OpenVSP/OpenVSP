MDC_PROTUB

Contents

The MDC_PROTUB is a set of identical protuberances on a body, laid out the way DATCOM lays them out with its PROTUB namelist: bumps, fairings, lugs and shoes of one type, at one station along the body, rolled to angles around it. It models things that stand off a body's surface, such as launch lugs and shoes, antennas, sensor blisters and fairings. A set is one type of protuberance at one axial station. A body with several kinds of protuberance, or protuberances at several stations, has an MDC_PROTUB for each, where DATCOM has one namelist for them all.

The controls carry the names and meanings of the namelist's inputs, so a set of protuberances can be entered as DATCOM has them. They stand on a body of circular cross section, which is an MDC_BOD with NAMELIST AXIBOD, or any body of that shape. The MDC_PROTUB does not read the body: it is told the body's radius where it stands. The datcom2vsp.py script, in the examples, reads a DATCOM input file and builds a model with an MDC_PROTUB for each protuberance set.

The MDC_PROTUB has one tab of its own, Design. The Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub tabs are described on their own pages. The Design tab sets

  • the type and where it stands;
  • a preset shape to start from;
  • the size of each of its members;
  • the number and roll angles of the protuberances in the set.

Quick Start

  1. Add the body, an MDC_BOD, and an MDC_PROTUB as its child. A new MDC_PROTUB is a small cylinder at X = 5, on the default MDC_BOD.
  2. Set XPROT to the station of the protuberance's center. The protuberance stands on the body there.
  3. Choose a Preset, the type you want, and press Set to size it, in proportion to the body.
  4. Change the size of each member as needed: LPROT, WPROT, HPROT and OPROT.
  5. Set NLOC, the number of protuberances, and, if they are not evenly spaced, turn on PHIPRO given and give each its roll angle.

Types of Protuberance

PTYPE is one of six types (Figure 1).

  • VCYL: a vertical cylinder, standing on the body, its axis out from the body. Its footprint is an ellipse LPROT along the body and WPROT across it.
  • HCYL: a horizontal cylinder, lying along the body, flat at its ends. Its section is an ellipse WPROT wide and HPROT high.
  • BLOCK: a box.
  • FAIRING: the streamlined half body of DATCOM, the top half of an ellipsoid LPROT long and WPROT wide at its flat base, and HPROT high above it, as the DATCOM manual defines it. DATCOM's own drawing of the geometry makes it half as high.
  • LUG: a launch lug, of four members: a base plate, two posts under the ends of a top plate, and the top plate.
  • SHOE: a launch shoe, of three members: a base plate, a web and a top plate, centered across the body.

Figure 1. The six types, each as its preset sizes it, on a body of radius 6.

A LUG or SHOE is built of members, each a box. Member 1 is the base plate of both. Member 2 is a SHOE's web or a LUG's first post, member 3 is a SHOE's top plate or the LUG's second post, and member 4 is a LUG's top plate. The posts of a LUG stand at the ends of the top plate, across the body, so the top plate's WPROT sets how far apart they are (Figure 2). The other types have one member. The Design tab has a block of sizes for each member, headed with its part in the type, such as Member 2: First Post, and greys those the type does not have.

Figure 2. The members of a LUG and of a SHOE, from behind the body.

Presets

The Preset choice and its Set button give the protuberance a shape. Choosing a preset changes nothing until Set is pressed. Default is the protuberance as new. Every other preset sets PTYPE and the sizes of every member the type has, in proportion to the body: the sizes in the table below, which are for a body of the radius given, scaled by RBODY over that radius. The locations, NLOC and PHIPRO, are kept. Use a preset as a starting point and change the sizes from there. Choosing a PTYPE on its own keeps the sizes of the members the two types share and gives each member it adds the sizes of a new one, with no offset; use a preset for a LUG or SHOE with its members stacked.

The VCYL, SHOE, BLOCK and FAIRING presets are the protuberances of DATCOM's sample body, which has a radius of 6; it has no HCYL or LUG, so those two are in proportion with them. The Small, Medium and Large Lug presets are launch lugs for a rocket of radius 5. The API call SetMdcPreset does the same.

Placing a Protuberance

A protuberance stands on the mold line, the surface of the body. DATCOM takes the body's radius there from the body, and so does an MDC_PROTUB that is a child of an MDC_BOD: each protuberance stands at the distance from the body's axis to its mold line under the protuberance's center, in the direction of its own roll angle, so a body that tapers, is elliptical or is cambered is followed. RBODY then shows the first protuberance's radius and is greyed. Any other MDC_PROTUB takes the mold line to be a cylinder of radius RBODY about the X axis; RBODY is not an input of DATCOM's namelist.

XPROT is the station of the protuberance's center, along XX from the origin, as DATCOM measures it. It is the X location of the XForm tab, shown again on the Design tab.

Each member is centered along the body at XPROT. It is LPROT long, along the body, WPROT wide, across it, and HPROT high, out from the body (Figure 3). OPROT is the height of the member's bottom above the mold line, zero by default, so a member with no offset sits on the body. A raised member, such as a LUG's top plate, floats above it. The presets stack the members of a LUG or a SHOE: each member's OPROT is the height of the members under it.

Figure 3. A BLOCK of length LPROT, width WPROT and height HPROT, raised by OPROT, on a body of radius RBODY, at the station XPROT.

A member with no offset stands with its flat bottom on the body's top line, the body's radius above its axis, as DATCOM draws it. It touches the body along that line, and on a round body the edges of its bottom stand clear of the body at its sides, by more the wider it is. A FAIRING's flat base is on the top line the same way. Its offset is zero, as DATCOM takes it, and its OPROT is greyed.

With Bury Root on, a member with no offset reaches down into the body instead, so that it meets the body all around its footprint rather than touching it along a line. It goes as far as the body passes under its sides at its two ends, or as far as a round body of the radius under its center passes under them, whichever is lower, and a tenth of its height further; a FAIRING's base is carried straight down as far. On a nose or a boattail the end over the narrower body decides it, and on an elliptical body the body is followed round to the member's sides. Where the body at an end is narrower than the member, the member reaches down to the body's axis. The part inside the body is not seen once the model is joined, and the outside is the same but for the gap under the sides, which is filled. A raised member, and an HCYL, which lies along the body, are not buried. Leave Bury Root off to match DATCOM's geometry; turn it on for a model to be joined, meshed or analyzed in OpenVSP, so that each protuberance meets the body with no gap under it.

The members of a LUG or SHOE are joined, with or without Bury Root. Members of one length that meet are one surface. Where members of different lengths meet exactly, one is buried in the other by half the other's height. No two surfaces touch exactly, so the surfaces intersect cleanly where the model is meshed or analyzed.

Locations

NLOC is the number of protuberances in the set, from 1 to 20, all the same. They are rolled around the body at the station XPROT, each at its angle PHIPRO. An angle is measured clockwise from the top looking forward, so 90 degrees is to the right (Figure 4). Unless PHIPRO given is on, the protuberances are evenly spaced around the body, the first at the top, and the angles are set from NLOC; the PHIPRO slider shows them but is greyed. With PHIPRO given on, each angle is set by hand. The selector below PHIPRO given chooses which one the slider shows. An angle that has not been set is zero, so that a protuberance added by raising NLOC is at the top until it is moved.

Figure 4. Three protuberances of a set, at PHIPRO 0, 90 and 225, from behind the body.

The protuberances of a set are all the same, so a change to a member applies to all of them. The angle rolls the whole protuberance, so a SHOE's base plate, web and top plate turn together.

Parameters

The parms are in the Design group but for XPROT, which is the XForm tab's X location. There is a PHIPRO for each of the NLOC protuberances, PHIPRO_1 to PHIPRO_n, and an LPROT, WPROT, HPROT and OPROT for each member the type has, LPROT_1 to LPROT_m: one member, or four for a LUG and three for a SHOE. They are made and deleted as NLOC and PTYPE change, at the next update.
