MDC_FIN

Contents

The MDC_FIN is a set of fin panels on a body, laid out the way DATCOM lays out a fin set with its FINSETn namelist: one planform given at up to ten span stations, one type of section, and up to eight identical panels rolled around the body. It suits fins, canards and tails, and any other set of identical panels set around a body. A fin set is one component; the fin sets FINSET1 to FINSET9 of a DATCOM input are separate components, so a body with canards and tail fins has two.

The controls carry the names and meanings of the namelist's inputs, so a fin set can be entered as DATCOM has it. The panels stand on a body: an MDC_FIN that is a child of an MDC_BOD takes the distance from the axis where its panels begin from the body, and any other is told it (see Panels). The datcom2vsp.py script, in the examples, reads a DATCOM input file and builds a model with an MDC_FIN for each fin set. An MDC_FIN can be converted to Wings, when the fins need the controls of a wing (see Convert to Wing).

The MDC_FIN has three tabs of its own: Fin Set, Planform and Section. The Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub tabs are described on their own pages.

  • The Fin Set tab sets the panels, their number, roll angles and dihedral, and the section type.
  • The Planform tab sets the planform at its span stations, and the flaps.
  • The Section tab sets the sections: HEX and ARC, NACA and USER.
  • Convert to Wing is a button at the top of the Fin Set tab.

Quick Start

  1. Add the body, an MDC_BOD, and an MDC_FIN as its child. A new MDC_FIN is four tapered panels, 1.5 chord at the root and 0.75 at the tip, 1 span, 5 percent thick diamonds, rolled to the top, right, bottom and left of the body at X = 8 to 9.5, on the default MDC_BOD.
  2. On the Fin Set tab, set NPANEL, the number of panels.
  3. On the Planform tab, set NSPAN, the number of span stations, and at each station its SSPAN, CHORD and XLE, or its SWEEP.
  4. On the Fin Set tab, choose the section type SECTYP, and on the Section tab give its dimensions.
  5. If the panels are not evenly spaced around the body, turn on PHIF given and give each its roll angle.

Panels

NPANEL is the number of panels in the set, from 1 to 8, all the same. A panel stands out from the body from its root, with its root chord parallel to the body's axis and the middle of the root chord on the body (Figure 1). DATCOM takes the body's radius there from the body, and so does an MDC_FIN that is a child of an MDC_BOD: each panel's root is at the distance from the body's axis to its mold line under the middle of the root chord, in the direction of the panel's own roll angle, so on an elliptical body the panels' roots are at different distances. RBODY then shows the first panel's and is greyed. Any other MDC_FIN puts every root RBODY from the axis; RBODY is not an input of DATCOM's namelist. If SSPAN_1, the span station of the root, is not zero, it is the root's distance from the axis and the body is not used (see Planform).

Each panel is rolled around the body to its angle PHIF, measured clockwise from the top looking forward, so 90 degrees is to the right (Figure 2). Unless PHIF given is on, the panels are evenly spaced around the body, the first at the top, and the angles are set from NPANEL; four panels are the cross of top, right, bottom and left. The PHIF slider shows them but is greyed. With PHIF given on, each angle is set by hand. The Panel selector chooses which panel's angle and dihedral the sliders show.

A panel's dihedral, GAM, turns it further about its root, positive in the direction in which PHIF increases, so the tip of a panel on the right goes down. A panel with no dihedral is in a plane through the axis. On a body that is not round, such a panel is not perpendicular to the body's surface.

Figure 2. Three evenly spaced panels, and two panels given a dihedral, seen from behind the body.

A section that is not symmetric top to bottom, such as a cambered NACA section or a HEX whose ZLOWER differs from its ZUPPER, is reflected, not rotated, to the panels on the left of the body, those at a PHIF between 180 and 360 degrees, and to a panel at the top. A pair of cambered panels on the left and right then both lift the same way: the camber is toward +Z+Z for panels at the sides, and toward +Y+Y at the top and bottom. This is as DATCOM has it.

Planform

The planform is given at up to ten span stations, NSPAN of them, and is straight between them (Figure 3). The Station selector chooses the station the sliders show; it is the same selector on the Planform and Section tabs.

  • SSPAN is the position of the station across the span, in the plane of the panel. Where SSPAN_1 is zero, the stations are measured from the root, which is on the body. Where it is not, they are measured from the axis, and SSPAN_1 is the position of the root. Station spans must increase.
  • CHORD is the chord there. A chord of zero makes a point, so a zero chord at the last station is a pointed tip.
  • XLE is the position of the chord's leading edge along the body. The root's is the station of the fin set, along XX from the origin as DATCOM measures it: it is the X location of the XForm tab, shown again as station 1's XLE. A later station's is measured from the root's leading edge, so the planform moves with the fin set.
  • SWEEP is the sweepback angle of the segment from a station to the next, measured from a line across the span, at the chord station STA of the segment: STA of 0 is at the leading edge, 1 is at the trailing edge, and STA of 0.5 is at the midchord (Figure 4). It sets where the next station's leading edge is. The last station has no segment after it, so it has no SWEEP or STA.

Figure 3. A panel of three stations. The leading edge of station 2 is set by SWEEP_1 and station 3's is given.

Each segment's leading edge is set one of two ways, by its sweep or by the position of its outboard station. By default it is the sweep, and the next station's XLE follows it: the sliders show what it works out to. Turn on XLE of next given at a station, and the next station's XLE is the input instead, and the SWEEP follows from it. The input that follows is greyed. The default STA of 1 and SWEEP of 0 make a panel with an unswept trailing edge, whatever its taper, as the default fin has.

Figure 4. The same sweep, 25 degrees, measured at three chord stations of the same root and tip chords. The line the sweep is measured on is blue.

Give the sweep of the edge whose sweep is known exactly: for a panel with an unswept trailing edge, SWEEP 0 and STA 1, and the leading edge follows from the chords. The planform of a small fin is sensitive to its sweep angle.

LER is the radius of the leading edge at each station, for HEX and ARC sections, and is zero by default: a sharp leading edge. It is described under Sections.

Flaps

CFOC is the ratio of flap chord to fin chord at each station, and makes plain trailing edge flaps (Figure 5). A flap is made on each segment whose two stations both have a nonzero CFOC. It runs from the segment's inboard to its outboard station, as deep as the CFOC at the station times the fin's chord there, and linearly between, on every panel of the set. A flap's deflection is not set here; the flaps are control surfaces, listed on the Sub tab, first named FLAP_1, FLAP_2 and so on, for the segment each is on. The component adds, sizes and deletes them as CFOC changes. A flap may be renamed; the component keeps track of it all the same.

A CFOC of zero at a station leaves it out of any flap. If CFOC_1 is 1 or more, the whole panel moves: there is no flap, and the later values are not used. This is the default, with CFOC of 1 at every station, so a new MDC_FIN has no flaps.

Figure 5. A fin of four stations whose CFOC of 0, 0.3, 0.25 and 0 makes one flap, on its middle segment.

Sections

SECTYP chooses one type of section for the whole fin set: HEX, ARC, NACA or USER. A HEX or ARC section is given at each station, so it may change across the span; a NACA or USER section is the same at every station, scaled by the chord. The Section tab has a block of inputs for each type, and greys those of the types not chosen.

HEX and ARC sections

A HEX section is a hexagon, OpenVSP's wedge airfoil (Figure 6). Its upper surface rises from the leading edge to a maximum thickness ZUPPER at the chord fraction LMAXU, stays at that thickness for a fraction of the chord LFLATU, and falls to the trailing edge, and the lower surface is the same with ZLOWER, LMAXL and LFLATL. ZUPPER and ZLOWER are heights above and below the chord line, as fractions of the chord. A LFLATU of zero, the default, makes a diamond. An ARC section is OpenVSP's biconvex airfoil: circular arcs above and below the chord line, ZUPPER and ZLOWER high at midchord. It has no LMAX or LFLAT. The defaults are a symmetric diamond, ZUPPER of 0.025 with its maximum at midchord.

ZLOWER, LMAXL and LFLATL each have a Rel/Abs switch. In Rel, the default, the value is its ratio to the upper surface's, so the section is symmetric and follows the upper surface when it changes. In Abs the value is the number entered, and the ratio follows it. Each switch is for the whole fin set. A ZLOWER that differs from ZUPPER makes a section that is not symmetric top to bottom.

Figure 6. A HEX section, an ARC section, and the HEX section with LER 0.03 and LER Type Keep Crest, each of chord 1. The sharp HEX is dashed on the last.

LER, the leading edge radius, is a DATCOM input whatever OpenVSP draws, and LER Type chooses what that is. Sharp, the default, keeps the leading edge sharp, as DATCOM's own drawing of the geometry does. Keep Crest and Keep Angle round it to a circle of radius LER: the airfoil's rounded leading edge, with LER over the chord as its radius. Either way the section keeps its chord, and its leading edge stays at XLE. Keep Crest keeps every input of the section exact, ZUPPER, LMAXU, LFLATU and the lower surface's, and refits the forward surfaces to meet the circle; Keep Angle keeps the forward surfaces' slope and moves the thickest points forward instead. Keep Crest suits a fin given as DATCOM gives it. The radius is held to about the smaller of ZUPPER and ZLOWER times the chord. LER and LER Type are greyed for a NACA or USER section.

NACA sections

The NACA section is chosen by NACASER, the series, and described by NACADES, the designation of the section as a number, as in DATCOM's NACA control card. The thickness is always the last two digits of the designation, in percent of chord, and may have decimals (Figure 7).

NACASER NACADES Example
4 Digit Camber in percent, its position in tenths, thickness. 2412
Modified 4 Digit As the 4 digit, and NACAMOD for the section's nose. 2412, 63
5 Digit Design lift, position of camber, thickness. 23012
Modified 5 Digit As the 5 digit, and NACAMOD. 23012, 63
1 Series 16, design lift coefficient in tenths, thickness. 16212
6 Series 6, position of minimum pressure in tenths, design lift coefficient in tenths, thickness. 64212
Supersonic Diamond Thickness in percent of chord. 6
Supersonic Arc Thickness in percent of chord. 6
Supersonic Hex Thickness in percent of chord. 6

The sections are OpenVSP's airfoils of those series. The 1 series is the 16 series, and the 6 series are those from 63 to 67. NACAMOD is the two digits of a modified series: the first is the leading edge radius, 0 for sharp and 6 for normal, and the second is the position of maximum thickness in tenths; it matters only to the modified series. NACASEA makes a 6 series section an "A" section, straight from 80 percent chord to the trailing edge, and NACAMEAN is the 6 series' mean line parameter, from 0.1 to 1. The supersonic sections are a diamond and a hexagon, OpenVSP's wedge airfoil, and an arc, OpenVSP's biconvex airfoil, with their thickness in NACADES, the position of maximum thickness in percent of chord NACAXMAX, and for the hexagon the length of its flat in percent of chord, NACAFLAT. Each input is greyed unless it applies to the series chosen.

USER sections

A USER section is an airfoil read from a file, the same at every station and scaled by the chord. The Read Airfoil button on the Section tab reads an airfoil file, .af or .dat, as the file airfoil of a Wing does, and its points are saved with the component. Until a file is read the section is a 5 percent thick biconvex section. A DATCOM user section, given by points as XCORD, MEAN and THICK, or XCORD, YUPPER and YLOWER, must be written as an airfoil file first.

Convert to Wing

The Convert to Wing button at the top of the Fin Set tab makes the equivalent Wing: the first panel is a Wing, and each other panel, and each copy of a panel that the fin set's symmetry makes, is a Clone of it, placed where that panel lies. They have the same chords, spans, sweeps and sections, and the flaps are the Wing's control surfaces. It asks whether to replace the MDC_FIN or add them beside it. Replace swaps the MDC_FIN for the Wing and its Clones, which take its place in the model, and cannot be undone; Add leaves the MDC_FIN as it is. Convert when the fins need what a Wing has, such as a different section at each station. A Wing's sweep stops at 89 degrees and a zero root chord is not a Wing's, so a fin set with either is a little off. The API call ConvertMdcFinToWing does the same.

Parameters

A fin set has one of each station input for each of its NSPAN stations, numbered from 1, as SSPAN_1 to SSPAN_n; a segment's, SWEEP_1 to SWEEP_n-1, are numbered by the station it starts from. A panel's, PHIF_1 to PHIF_n for NPANEL panels, are numbered by the panel. They are made and deleted as NSPAN and NPANEL change, at the next update. XLE is from station 2 on, XLE_2 to XLE_n: the root's leading edge is the XForm tab's X location. All the other parms are in the Design group.
