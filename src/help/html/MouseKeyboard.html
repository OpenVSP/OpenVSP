Mouse and Keyboard

Contents

Most of OpenVSP is driven through its windows' visible controls, but a good deal of it also answers to the mouse and keyboard in ways the controls do not show. This page gathers all of them in one place: how the mouse moves the 3D view and how to change that, the keyboard shortcuts, and what the Parm controls, lists and trees do beyond their obvious use, including where a Parm can be dragged and dropped.

On macOS, Option is the key other systems call Alt, and Cmd stands in for Ctrl in many shortcuts. This page writes Alt for Alt or Option.

Windows

The Geom Browser sits in a panel along the right edge of the 3D view, over the view and its full height. The button at the right of its title hides it, and the small tab left at the right edge of the view, Model > Geometry or the toolbar's Geometry browser button brings it back.

File > Preferences > General > Windows > Screens sets how the other screens are shown. With Free Windows, the default, each is a window of its own. With Captive Windows, each is a window inside the main window, over the 3D view: drag its title bar to move it, drag its edges and corners to size it, and close it with the button at the right of its title bar. A captive window stays inside the main window, keeps its size as the main window is resized, and comes to the top when it is clicked or typed in. A change moves the open screens in or out at once and is kept for the next start.

The Geom windows share one place, their right edges against the Geom Browser's panel, and each opens where the last one was left. A window made taller keeps its controls their size: its plot, 3D view, list or table, where it has one, takes the extra height.

Toolbar

The toolbar under the menu bar puts the commands used most a click away. Each button does exactly what its menu command does, and its tooltip names the command and its key. A button for something that is on, such as Pick mode, the rotation, the view layout, the axes or the border, is shown pressed in. Every other button has a faint raised edge, raised fully while the mouse is over it.

Moving the view

Out of the box the mouse moves the view as it always has in OpenVSP:

Press Does
Left drag Rotate
Alt + left drag Pan
Right drag Pan
Middle drag Zoom
Ctrl + left drag (or Cmd + left drag) Zoom
Left + right drag Zoom
z held + left drag Roll about the view direction
Wheel Zoom, about the point under the pointer

The mouse buttons can instead follow the habits of another program; see Schemes. Whatever the scheme, a drag acts on the viewport it starts in, and the action is chosen when the drag starts: pressing or releasing a key part way through does not switch from rotating to panning. Esc during a drag puts the view back as it was at the press.

Ball and turntable

A rotate drag turns the view one of two ways.

  • Ball: the model turns freely, as if it were inside a ball being rolled. Any orientation can be reached. What the ball does is set in the preferences: the Trackball (the default), the Rounded Arcball, OpenVSP Classic (the rotation of OpenVSP 3.55 and earlier), or the Tumbler.
  • Turntable: horizontal motion turns the model about the vertical Z axis and vertical motion tips it toward or away from you, so Z always stays upright on the screen. It suits aircraft, which have a natural up.

View > Turntable Rotation (t) and View > Ball Rotation (b) switch between them at any time; the preferences set which one OpenVSP starts with. Switching to the turntable first turns the view about the screen's axis until Z points up. View > Z Up (z), or a tap of z on its own with no mouse button down, does that at any time.

The four balls differ in what a drag does:

  • Rounded Arcball: the press point is grabbed on a sphere and follows the mouse, so a drag that comes back to where it started puts the view back exactly. Starting a drag outside the sphere turns the view about the line of sight instead; the outline of the sphere is drawn while it turns. Its size and speed are preferences.
  • OpenVSP Classic: OpenVSP's rotation through version 3.55, kept exactly at the default Speed of 0.5. Each mouse movement turns the view on its own, so moving the mouse in a circle and back to where it started leaves the view turned.
  • Trackball: Bell's trackball surface, as in SGI's tools, measured from the press, so a drag that comes back to where it started puts the view back exactly. Near the middle of the view it turns like OpenVSP Classic; farther out it turns the model less and less, tipping it on a move toward or away from the middle and rolling it on a move around.
  • Tumbler: each mouse movement turns the view about the screen axis across it, by a fixed amount per point of movement. Any orientation can be reached, but the result depends on the path: moving the mouse in a small circle rolls the model. ParaView, EnSight, Pointwise and STAR-CCM+ turn this way.

What the view turns about

The view turns about the rotation center. View > Set Rotation Center (r) waits for a click on the model and moves the rotation center there; View > Center (c) and Center All (C) put it at the middle of the selected or all Geoms. With Rotate About set to Point under cursor in the preferences, each drag instead turns about the surface point under the press, while the rotation center itself stays where it was. A marker shows the point being turned about.

Zooming

The wheel zooms about the point under the pointer by default; the preferences can make it zoom about the middle of the view, reverse its direction, and scale its step with the amount scrolled, which suits trackpads. A zoom drag zooms about the middle of the view by default.

Zoom in and Zoom out can also be bound to a button, so that a press zooms one step and holding the button keeps zooming; a mouse's Back and Forward side buttons suit them. See Mouse button preferences.

View > Zoom Box (w) arms a one-time zoom box: the next left drag draws a rectangle, and the view zooms so the rectangle fills it. Esc gives the box up.

Saved views

F1 to F4 recall four saved views; Shift with F1 to F4 saves the current view into that slot. The saved views last until OpenVSP closes. F5 to F12 show the standard views: top, front, left, left isometric, bottom, rear, right and right isometric.

Rotatrix trackball

When OpenVSP is built with the Rotatrix SDK and the Rotatrix host is running, the Rotatrix trackball moves the view. The trackball icon at the bottom left of the main window is lit while it is connected; its tooltip gives the status, and a click opens its preferences. View > Rotatrix Trackball (d) turns it on or off. Rotatrix computes the motion with its own controls and chooses the point to turn about itself; View > Lock Rotatrix Pivot (l) asks it to keep turning about the current one, and again frees it. In the turntable mode OpenVSP keeps Z upright as Rotatrix turns the view.

Picking and selecting

Several tools ask for a point on the model and then wait for a click: Set Rotation Center (r), View Normal To (n), the rulers, probes and protractors of the Measure window, routing points, and the points of a Fit Model target. While one is waiting, a left press goes to it first, unless a binding listed before the one that gives presses to tool picks takes it: in OpenVSP's own scheme, a left press with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, or with z held, still zooms, pans or rolls.

There are two ways to select a Geom by clicking on it:

  • Pick mode, on the Geom Browser's Pick button or Edit > Toggle Pick Mode (p). While it is on, clicking a Geom makes it the only one selected.
  • In every scheme but OpenVSP's own, a left click selects the Geom under it without any mode: on its own it makes that Geom the only one selected, with Shift it adds it, with Ctrl or Cmd it adds or removes it, and a click on the background clears the selection.

Esc in the 3D window, when nothing is being dragged, clears the selection.

Box selection in Fit Model

While the Fit Model window is open, a left drag with Shift held, or any left drag with its Select Region button on, draws a rectangle and selects the target points inside it. This comes before every other use of the left button and cannot be changed in the preferences.

3D View preferences

File > Preferences opens the preferences. Changes take effect at once, so they can be tried with the window still open. Revert puts back what was set when the window opened, Defaults sets OpenVSP's own, and Close keeps the changes.

Mouse button preferences

The Mouse Buttons tab shows what each mouse press does, and lets any of it be changed.

At the top are three settings for all the bindings:

  • Drag Starts: how far the mouse must move before a press counts as a drag. Until then it may still be a click, so one button can drag and click to different effect, as a middle drag rotating and a middle click setting the rotation center. OpenVSP's own scheme starts every drag at once.
  • Release One of Two: with two buttons held together, as left and right zoom in OpenVSP's scheme, letting go of one either goes on with what the other button does, as OpenVSP always has, or stops, as CAD programs do.
  • Alt + Left: Alt with the left button can act as the middle button, for a mouse or trackpad without one. It applies only to presses no binding uses as they are.

Below them is the list of bindings, tried from the top: the first that fits a press acts. A binding shown in red can never act, because those above it take every press it would. Add puts a copy of the selected binding above it, Delete removes it, and Up and Down move it. The selected binding's fields are:

Field What it means
Action Rotate, Roll, Pan, Zoom, Zoom in, Zoom out, Zoom box, Rotation center, Select Geom, or Tool picks only, which gives the press to a waiting tool and does nothing else.
On Whether the binding acts on a drag or on a click.
Button The button that must be down: Left, Middle, Right, or a mouse's Back or Forward side button.
With A second button that must be down with it, for a chord, or None.
Shift, Ctrl, Alt, Cmd Whether each key must be up, down, or may be either.
Other Buttons Whether buttons the binding does not need may also be down.
Held Key A letter or digit that must be held, such as z for roll.
Where Anywhere, only on the model, or only on the background.
Tool Picks Whether a waiting tool pick takes the press first.

Zoom in and Zoom out zoom one wheel notch, about the pointer or the middle of the view as Wheel Zooms is set. They act at the press, and again ten times a second while the button is held, however the mouse moves; On does not apply to them. A mouse's Back and Forward buttons bound to Zoom out and Zoom in zoom the view without the wheel.

Rather than setting the fields by hand, press in the Try a Press box with the buttons and keys wanted: it shows what that press does now, and Use This Press for the Selected Binding copies it into the selected binding.

Schemes

A scheme sets the mouse buttons, and the rotation and zoom settings, to those of another program, so a user of that program finds the view moving as expected. None is an exact copy of its program. The Scheme menu lists each with other programs that work the same way, and its tooltip lists what the buttons do.

In every scheme but OpenVSP's own, a left click selects the Geom under it, a left press goes first to a waiting tool pick, a press becomes a drag after the mouse moves 4 points, letting go of one of two buttons stops, the wheel and zoom drags go by distance, and on macOS Cmd works wherever Ctrl does.

Parm controls

Each slider or value box has a button with the Parm's name to its left.

  • Clicking the name button opens the Parm window: its description and limits, its attributes, and the links and advanced links it takes part in. The name is drawn in dark magenta when the Parm has attributes.
  • Dragging the name button picks the Parm up, to drop where a Parm is wanted; see Dragging and dropping Parms.
  • A value box takes arithmetic: typing 180/6 sets 30. It understands + - * / ^, brackets and exponents, but no functions or names. A value applies on Enter or when the box loses the focus.
  • The small buttons at either end of a slider change its range. When the value is inside the range, the inward arrows halve the range about the value. When the value is at an end, the outward arrow extends the range by half and moves the value to the new end. A bar means the Parm's own limit has been reached. Typing a value outside the slider's range recenters the range on it.
  • Clicking on a slider's track jumps the value there, and the arrow keys nudge a slider that has the focus by one hundredth of its range.
  • Holding Alt while dragging a slider of a Geom's placement turns on Snap To's collision detection for that change, so the part stops at the clearance set in Model > Snap To.

Dragging and dropping Parms

Dragging a Parm's name button and dropping it is the quickest way to tell a window which Parm is meant. The drop can land anywhere in the area named below, not only on its Parm picker.

Window Drop on Does
Model > Adv Link the Input or Output area Adds the Parm as an input or output variable; see Advanced Parameter Linking.
Model > Link the Parm A or Parm B area Sets that end of the link.
Model > Design Variables the Pick tab Adds the Parm as a design variable.
Model > Fit Model the Pick Vars or Var Tree tab Adds the Parm as a fit variable.
Model > Variable Presets the Group tab Adds the Parm to the current group.
Model > Snap To the window Sets the Parm to move for collision detection.
Model > Attribute Explorer the Parm reference field Sets a Parm reference attribute.

A dropped Parm is its ID as text, so it can also be dropped into any text box, such as an advanced link's code or a script, to paste the ID. On Linux it is also left for a middle-click paste.

The edit curve and blade curve plots take a drag of their points, and the Wave Drag window's area plot takes a drag of its slicing line.

Lists and trees

  • Renaming: in the lists of names (Geoms, sets, links, advanced links, attributes, modes, measures, CFD sources, FEA parts and properties, variable presets, user Parms and more), a double-click on a name, or Enter or F2 with it selected, edits it in place. Enter or a click elsewhere keeps the new name. In the FEA structure's part list a double-click opens the part instead, and Enter or F2 renames it.
  • Advanced Link: a double-click on an input or output variable copies its name to the clipboard, ready to paste into the code.
  • Attributes: a double-click on an attribute in an attribute editor, on any tab, opens it in the Attribute Explorer.
  • Column widths: in lists with column headings, dragging the line between two columns sets their width.
  • Selecting several: Shift-click selects a range and Ctrl-click adds or removes one; dragging extends the selection. In the Geom Browser Ctrl is Ctrl even on macOS, and Alt-click also selects every Geom below the one clicked.
  • Geom Browser icons: the eye shows or hides, and the surface icon steps the draw type through wire, hidden, shaded, textured and none. Both act on the whole selection when the clicked Geom is in it, and on the Geoms below a collapsed one; clicking the Vehicle's acts on every Geom. Names in italic are made automatically and cannot be renamed.
  • Trees answer the arrow keys: up and down move, Shift extends, left and right collapse and expand, and Space selects.
  • Tables of values, such as the Attribute Explorer's: typing starts editing a cell, a double-click edits it, and Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V (Cmd on macOS) copy and paste a block as text separated by tabs.

Keyboard shortcuts

Ctrl is Cmd on macOS for the menu shortcuts; Cut, Copy and Paste answer Ctrl there as well. The menu shortcuts work from every window, but a key without Ctrl, Cmd or Alt types into a text box that is being typed in.

Key Does
Ctrl+S File > Save
Ctrl+Z Edit > Undo Parameter Change
Ctrl+X, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V Edit > Cut, Copy and Paste the selected Geoms
Ctrl+A Edit > Select All
p Edit > Toggle Pick Mode

The View menu's keys are listed under Keyboard in the 3D window.

Other windows have keys of their own:

  • Attribute Explorer: Ctrl+X, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Delete, as its buttons show.
  • Attribute text and Vehicle Notes: Enter keeps the text; Shift+Enter or Ctrl+Enter starts a new line.
  • Esc in any window, when nothing there uses it, clears the selection.
