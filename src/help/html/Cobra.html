Cobra

Contents

The Cobra is a forebody described by a handful of dimensions. Its nose is a super-ellipse in planform and in profile; its aft body runs straight back from the nose, tangent to it, to a flat base; and every cross section is a super-ellipse whose upper and lower halves may differ, its corners filling out from the nose to the base. It suits blunt-based bodies such as missile and hypersonic forebodies, canopies and fairings, where a few numbers should set the whole shape.

The shape is NASA's COBRA (Co-Optimization of Blunt-body Re-entry Analysis) body, defined at NASA Ames Research Center as a parametric family of lifting entry vehicles and used to optimize mid lift-to-drag aeroshells for Mars entry [1, 2].

The Cobra has one tab of its own, Design. The Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub tabs are described on their own pages.

Quick Start

  1. Add a Cobra. A new one is a hemisphere of radius 1 on a cylinder of length 1, closed by a flat base.
  2. Place it with the XForm tab.
  3. Set the nose's Length, Width, Upper Height and Lower Height, and PowX for the fullness of the nose.
  4. Set the aft body's Length, and the Width and heights of the base, and the cross section exponents.

Planform and Profile

The nose runs from its tip, at the origin, to the station a nose Length aft, xNx_N, where it reaches its full half-width Width and half-heights Upper Height and Lower Height (Figure 1). Seen from above its outline is the super-ellipse

|x−xNxN|px+|ywN|px=1 \left|\frac{x - x_N}{x_N}\right|^{p_x} + \left|\frac{y}{w_N}\right|^{p_x} = 1

where wNw_N is the nose Width and pxp_x is PowX; seen from the side, its upper and lower outlines are the same curve with the heights in place of wNw_N. PowX 2 gives an elliptical nose, larger values a blunter one, and 1 a pointed one with straight sides. Close to the tip the surface lies slightly inside this outline, which gives the tip a small point: with the default PowX of 2, by up to 5 percent of the width, within the first 5 percent of the nose Length (Figure 1).

The aft body ends in the base, an aft Length behind the nose station, with half-width Width and half-heights Upper Height and Lower Height. Its sides, top and bottom are straight lines from the edges of the base, each tangent to the nose's outline. An aft body narrower than the nose meets the nose just aft of its widest station; a wider one, ahead of it; one of the same width runs straight back from it. The body is the nose Length plus the aft Length long, and Rot Origin(X) on the XForm tab is a fraction of that.

Figure 1. A Cobra from above and from the side: nose Length 2, Width 1, Upper Height 0.8, Lower Height 0.5; aft Length 4, Width 0.5, Upper Height 0.5, Lower Height 0.2; PowX 2.

Either length may be zero. With no aft length the body is the nose alone, closed flat at its widest station. With no nose length it is the aft body alone, running straight from the nose's section to the base, and its front is left open.

Cross Sections

Each cross section is a super-ellipse, its upper and lower halves separate:

|yw|p+|zh|p=1 \left|\frac{y}{w}\right|^{p} + \left|\frac{z}{h}\right|^{p} = 1

where ww is the half-width and hh the upper or lower half-height at that station, read from the outlines above. The exponent pp is PowNU (upper) or PowNL (lower) over the nose. From the farther aft of the two points where the aft body's sides and its top (or bottom) leave the nose, or from the nose's widest station if both lie ahead of it, it changes smoothly, as a half cosine, to PowAU or PowAL at the base (Figure 2). An exponent of 2 gives an ellipse, larger values fill out the corners toward a rectangle, and 1 gives a diamond.

Figure 2. Cross sections of the Cobra of Figure 1, seen from the front: the nose's widest station, with exponent 2, and the base, with exponent 4, and stations between them.

Nose ULSymm and Aft ULSymm make the lower half the same as the upper, taking its height and exponent; the lower controls are then greyed. Both are on by default.

Parameters

Every width and height is measured from the axis: Width is a half-width, and the two heights are the extents above and below the axis, so a section's full height is their sum.

The exponents run from 1 to 10. All the parms are in the Design group.
