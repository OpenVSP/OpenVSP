Clone

Contents

The Clone component shows the shape of another component, its original, placed by the Clone's own XForm. A Clone stores no shape of its own: every change to the original appears at once in each of its Clones. Use Clones for parts that are the same, such as the engines of a multi-engine aircraft, a row of seats or the propellers of a multirotor, so that each design change is made once.

A Clone always takes its original's shape, at the original's scale, with the original's tessellation and end caps. Everything else -- where it sits, its symmetry, its sets, its color, its mass and drag settings and more -- it either takes from the original or keeps as its own, as chosen on the Clone tab.

Quick Start

  1. Select the component, or several, in the Geom browser and click Clone. Give a name suffix, _Clone by default, and click Clone.
  2. Each new Clone sits on top of its original. Move it with its XForm tab.
  3. Edit the original. Every Clone follows.

Making Clones

The Clone button of the Geom browser makes one Clone for each selected component and keeps the hierarchy among them: a Clone of a child is made a child of the Clone of its parent. The top Clone of each group is placed beside its original in the tree, starts at the original's placement, and has its own transformation, free to move. The Clones below it copy their originals' placement relative to their parents, so moving the top Clone moves the whole group (Figure 1). The Clones also take their originals' display settings.

Figure 1. The Geom browser after a pod and its child wing were cloned together: Pod_Clone sits beside Pod and carries Wing_Clone, which has none of Wing's children. The Clones' names are in italics because they follow their originals' names.

A Clone added from the Add menu has no original yet. On its first update it takes its parent as its original, so select the component to be copied before adding it; it starts with its own transformation at zero. An original can also be chosen afterwards on the Clone tab.

A Clone does not copy its original's children. To copy a component together with its children, select all of them and use the Clone button.

The Original

Original on the Clone tab lists the components a Clone can copy: any component except the Clone itself, the components attached below it, and any whose chain of Clones leads back to it. A Clone of a Clone shows the shape at the end of the chain.

What a Clone Copies

The shape, with the original's scale, its tessellation (Num_U, Num_W) and its end caps are always the original's; their controls are greyed on the Clone, and so is Scale: a Clone has no size of its own. The rest is chosen under Copy From Original. Each item checked is copied on every update, and its controls are greyed on the Clone; an item unchecked is the Clone's own and can be edited. Unchecking an item keeps the last copied values as the starting point.

Copy From Original Default What is copied
Transformation off Location, rotation, rotation origin and the Abs/Rel choice.
Attachment on The attachment to the parent and its coordinates.
Symmetry and Flip on The symmetry; the flip planes combine with the Clone's own.
Set Membership on Membership of the user's sets.
Color and Material on The color, the material and the textures.
Negative Volume on The CFD Mesh negative volume setting.
Mass Properties on Density, priority, thin shell and the specified mass, CG and inertia.
Parasite Drag on The Parasite Drag settings used for the Clone.
Subsurfaces on The original's subsurfaces.
Joint Deflection on A Hinge's Translate and Rotate (see below).
Rotor Rotation on A propeller's Rotate (see below).

Symmetry is copied by default because the symmetric copies of the original are part of its shape. The copied symmetry mirrors the Clone where the Clone is placed: a Clone moved aft of a nacelle mirrored in Y=0Y = 0 makes a second pair, and a Clone moved onto Y=0Y = 0, such as a center engine, shows its two halves on top of each other (Figure 2). For a single Clone on the mirror plane, uncheck Symmetry and Flip and then turn off the Clone's own symmetry, which keeps the copied setting until changed. The Clone's own Flip planes combine with the original's while Symmetry and Flip is checked, so the same plane set on both cancels. To make a Clone the mirror image of its original, set the mirror plane on the Clone's Flip.

Figure 2. A nacelle mirrored in Y=0Y = 0 and two Clones moved aft of it, the second onto Y=0Y = 0. The second Clone's two coincident halves in (a) show darker than the single nacelle in (b).

Set membership covers the user's sets only; whether a Clone is shown is its own.

A Clone's subsurfaces are copies of the original's that keep their identity as the original changes, so control surface groups and scripts that refer to them keep working. Unchecking Subsurfaces keeps the copies as the Clone's own, to edit, and lets more be added. Checking it again picks up the same copies rather than adding new ones.

A Clone always keeps its own name (unless named after the original), parent, children, attributes, CFD sources and FEA structures.

Naming

With Name After Original checked, the default, a Clone is named after its original with Suffix appended, and the name follows the original's when it is renamed. The name on the Gen tab is greyed while this is checked. The Geom browser shows such a name in italics and will not rename it. Uncheck it to give the Clone a name of its own; the current name is kept as the starting point.

Clones of Hinges and Propellers

A Clone of a Hinge is a joint too: its own children ride on its joint, just as the Hinge's children ride on the Hinge. The Hinge decides which motions are allowed and their limits. With Joint Deflection checked the Clone takes the Hinge's deflection; unchecked, the Clone's own Translate and Rotate in the Joint group pose it, so two Clones of one Hinge can sit at different angles. Rng sets a slider's range to the Hinge's limits.

A Clone of a Propeller takes the propeller's Rotate while Rotor Rotation is checked; unchecked, the Rotate in the Rotor group places the Clone's blades, so Clones of one rotor can turn apart in phase. See Clones of a Propeller for counter-rotating rotors.

Clones in Analyses

A Clone takes part in analyses as a component of its original's kind. A Clone of a wing can be the reference wing of VSPAERO and Parasite Drag, with the wing's span, area and mean aerodynamic chord; a Clone of a propeller is a rotor to VSPAERO and to an Auxiliary rotor zone; an Auxiliary component hung from a Clone of a Gear measures against the Clone; a Clone of a Routing component is weighed by Mass Properties as a route. A Clone of a mesh, a point cloud, a wireframe or a polygon mesh is placed as one piece and has no symmetry; its symmetry controls are greyed.

Deleting an Original

When a component that has Clones is deleted or cut, OpenVSP asks what becomes of its Clones:

Choice Result
Cancel Nothing is deleted.
Leave Empty The Clones stay where they are, copying nothing.
Replace Each Clone is replaced by a full copy of what it was copying (see below).
Delete Too, or Cut Too The Clones go with the original, and so do any Clones of those Clones.

A Clone left empty shows nothing, keeps its name and stops naming itself after an original; choose an original for it again on the Clone tab. The same happens, with a message, when a file is opened whose Clone has lost its original, or when a Clone's original is moved to below the Clone in the tree.

Replacing a Clone with a Copy

Replace With Copy Of Original turns a Clone into an ordinary, independent component: a full copy of the component at the end of its chain of Clones. The copy takes the Clone's place in the tree and its ID, so attached components, links, design variables and control surface groups that referred to the Clone keep working. It keeps what the Clone showed -- its name, placement, attachment, symmetry and flip, sets, mass and negative volume settings, the parasite drag settings it used, color and material, display settings, joint deflection and rotor angle -- and the Clone's subsurfaces, CFD sources, FEA structures and attributes.

A Clone of a polygon mesh cannot be replaced. Some components place their own shape from other components, such as a route or a conformal component; their copy may not land where the Clone stood, and a message says so.

Shared Tabs

The Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub tabs are common to every component; see Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub-Surfaces. Controls copied from the original are greyed on them.

Scripting

The OpenVSP API makes and manages Clones with these functions:

Call Purpose
CloneGeomVec Clone a group of components, keeping their hierarchy, as the Clone button does.
SetGeomCloneOriginal, GetGeomCloneOriginal Set or read a Clone's original; an empty ID clears it.
SetGeomCloneNameSuffix, GetGeomCloneNameSuffix The suffix of automatic naming.
FindGeomClones The Clones of a set of components.
ReplaceCloneGeom Replace a Clone with a copy of its original.
DeleteGeom, DeleteGeomVec, CutGeomToClipboard Take a CLONE_DELETE_TYPE that says what becomes of the Clones.

AddGeom( "CLONE" ) adds a Clone. The Copy From Original switches are parms in the group Behavior: CloneXForm, CloneAttach, CloneSym, CloneSets, CloneAppearance, CloneNegativeVolume, CloneMassProps, CloneParasiteDrag, CloneSubSurfs, CloneJoint and CloneRotate, with AutoName for Name After Original. A Clone's own joint deflection is JointTranslate and JointRotate in the group Hinge, and its own rotor angle is Rotate in the group Rotor.
