Propeller

Contents

The Propeller component models propellers and rotors. It builds one blade from cross sections and a set of spanwise curves, then places a copy of that blade for each blade of the rotor. Each copy is placed by a chain of rigid motions: the blade's spacing, the rotation of the rotor, the precone, and four hinges -- flap, lag, feather and fold. The flap, lag and feather can give every blade the same angle, give each blade its own angle, or vary each blade's angles with its azimuth as a harmonic series, as on a helicopter rotor; the fold takes one angle, or one per blade.

Quick Start

Both start from a preset at the top of the Design tab: choose it under Preset and click Set. A preset replaces the sections, the blade curves, the pitch, PsiRef and the Y rotation, set in the frame the propeller is placed in. Everything else keeps its value, so a preset is meant for a new propeller.

A propeller

  1. Choose Preset Propeller. A new propeller already has this blade. YRot is 0: the thrust points along the model's −X-X, so a tractor propeller on the nose of an aircraft whose nose points along −X-X needs no further rotation.
  2. Place it with the XForm tab.
  3. Set Diameter, Num Blades and the pitch, Beta 3/4. Normal rotation is clockwise seen from behind, looking forward; check Rev for the opposite direction.
  4. Shape the blade with the curves on the Blade tab and the airfoils on the XSec tab.

A helicopter main rotor

  1. Choose Preset Helicopter Rotor. The blade is a NACA 0012 of constant chord 0.06R0.06R, twisted from 0 at the root to −8∘-8^\circ at the tip; Feather drives the pitch, at 8∘8^\circ. YRot is 90, so the thrust points up, along +Z+Z, and PsiRef is 270, so ψ=0\psi = 0 lies over the tail (+X+X) and blade 1 advances on the right (ψ=90∘\psi = 90^\circ at +Y+Y); the blades turn counterclockwise seen from above.
  2. Place it with the XForm tab.
  3. Set Diameter, Num Blades and the collective pitch, Feather. Feather must drive the pitch for the blades to take cyclic or individual pitch (see Pitch).
  4. Shape the blade as for a propeller.

Coning, flapping and cyclic pitch are set on the Motion tab; see Harmonic Blade Motion.

Axes and Rotation Direction

The propeller is built in its own axes, which the XForm tab places in the model (Figure 1).

  • XX is the shaft, pointing downstream. The thrust points along −X-X.
  • YY is radial. Blade 1 lies along +Y+Y when Rotate and PsiRef are zero.
  • ZZ completes the right-handed set. For normal rotation the blades turn from +Y+Y toward −Z-Z, so the leading edge of blade 1 faces −Z-Z and the rotation vector Ω\Omega points along −X-X, the thrust direction.

The factor ϵ\epsilon is +1+1 for normal rotation and −1-1 when Rev is checked (see Reverse Rotation).

Figure 1. The propeller axes on the default propeller. Heavy edges are the trailing edges; Rotate ψ\psi turns blade 1 from +Y+Y, and ϕ2\phi_2 places blade 2.

Modeling Mode

Prop Mode chooses what is built.

Mode Surfaces
Blades One surface per blade.
Both The blades, then one disk surface.
Disk Only the disk: a flat circle of the propeller's diameter in the plane of rotation.

The disk is a transparent surface for analyses that model the rotor as an actuator disk. DegenGeom gives a disk as its center, diameter and normal, with no plates or sticks.

Prop Mode also decides what analyses receive. VSPAERO models a disk as an actuator disk, set up on its Disk tab, and blades as lifting surfaces, which its Propeller tab turns in an unsteady analysis. A disk is also much quicker to update, since no blade is lofted. Lay out an aircraft with many propellers in Disk mode, and use Blades where the blades themselves matter.

Blade Direction Marker sizes the rotation arrow to Fraction of the radius instead of the axis length.

Construct X/C and Feather Axis X/C do different jobs.

  • Construct X/C shapes the blade. It picks the point of every section that lies on the blade's construction line. Each section is twisted about that point, and Rake, Skew, Sweep, Axial and Tangential move it. With none of those offsets, the construction line is a radial line, and every section's Construct X/C point lies on it.
  • Feather Axis X/C places the blade without changing its shape. The whole blade moves, along the root chord, until the root section's Feather Axis X/C point lies on the feather axis. Feather Offset then moves the blade and its feather axis together off the shaft, along ZZ for normal rotation and −Z-Z for reverse: toward the trailing edge of an unpitched root. The direction does not turn with the root's twist or with Feather.

Both default to 0.5. With the defaults and no offsets, the construction line is the feather axis, so the sections twist about it. With no offsets but different values, the construction line is parallel to the feather axis, cr|fa−fc|c_r\,|f_a - f_c| from it along the root chord, where crc_r is the root chord and faf_a, fcf_c are Feather Axis X/C and Construct X/C. Both values are usually between 0 (the leading edge) and 1 (the trailing edge).

Pitch: Feather and Beta 3/4

The blade's pitch is set either by the Feather angle or by Beta 3/4, the pitch at three quarters of the radius. The two are related through the twist at r/R=0.75r/R = 0.75:

Beta3/4=Feather+Twist(0.75) \mathrm{Beta}_{3/4} = \mathrm{Feather} + \mathrm{Twist}(0.75)

where the twist term is zero if the blade starts outboard of 0.75R0.75 R. The radio buttons (UseBeta34Flag) choose which one is set; the other is shown as an output. Beta 3/4 drives by default. While it drives, every blade takes the same Feather, whatever the blade motion mode; any per-blade and harmonic feathers are kept, greyed, and are not used. To give the blades individual or cyclic pitch, select Feather: the feather then follows the blade motion mode like the flap and lag.

Cross Sections

The XSec tab holds the blade's cross sections. Each section sits at a Radius given as a fraction r/Rr/R of the tip radius; the last section is always at the tip, r/R=1r/R = 1. The first section's radius is the root of the blade and sets the hub diameter used by analyses.

A section's type sets its airfoil family, not its proportions. The Chord, Thick and CLi curves on the Blade tab set the chord, the thickness and the design lift coefficient at every radius, and the section's own values for these are greyed. CLi sets the camber of the families that have one: the NACA 5-digit, 5-digit modified, 16-series and 6-series take it directly, and the NACA 4-digit and 4-digit modified take it as the maximum camber that gives that design lift coefficient at the section's camber position. That camber comes from thin airfoil theory, by which the 4-digit mean line's design lift coefficient is proportional to its maximum camber; the relation was derived for OpenVSP so that a blade could be described by CLi. Other families, such as the ellipse, biconvex and wedge, take the thickness but have no camber for CLi to set.

Between two sections, the blade's sections are made in one of two ways.

  • When the two sections are of the same family, OpenVSP builds a true airfoil of that family at every radius, from its equations, with the thickness and CLi of the curves there. The family's other parameters, such as the camber position, are interpolated between the two sections.
  • When the families differ, or the sections are File, CST or Edit Curve sections, OpenVSP can only blend the two shapes. The blend is split into a camber line and a thickness distribution, and the thickness is scaled to the Thick curve while the camber line is kept, so CLi has no effect there.

So use as few families as you can, and families defined by equations. A root and a tip section are enough; the curves on the Blade tab carry the variation of chord, thickness and camber along the span, so more sections are needed only to change the family. Two sections suit a blade of one family; three, a blade with a round or elliptical cuff at the root; four, a blade that changes from one family to another, with a section at each end of the change. The default propeller has an ellipse at the root, as many propellers do, and 16-series sections from r/R=0.4r/R = 0.4 to the tip. Between the root and 0.4 its sections are blends; from 0.4 out they are true 16-series airfoils at each radius's thickness and CLi. File and CST airfoils can only be scaled and blended, never regenerated: their thickness follows the Thick curve, but CLi does not change them.

The Modify tab trims, closes, caps and flaps the active section as on a wing; see The Modify Tab. An Abs closure keeps its thickness in model units at every radius as the chord changes along the blade, while a Rel closure stays a fixed fraction of the local chord; a trailing edge of constant thickness is set as Abs.

Blade Curves

The Blade tab edits curves of each quantity against r/Rr/R (Figure 4). Each curve can be Linear, Spline (PCHIP) or Cubic Bezier. The first and last points are pinned to the root and tip. Approximate All converts every curve to a cubic Bezier. Reset Thick rebuilds the Thick curve from the thickness of each section.

Figure 4. The Curve Editor of the Blade tab showing the default Chord curve, a Cubic Bezier, with the root point selected.

In the plot, the black diamonds are the points the curve passes through, and the green circles are the Bezier handles, joined to their points by thin lines; a Linear or PCHIP curve has only diamonds. The selected point is a yellow square, and its row of the Control Points table is labeled in yellow. Points can be dragged in the plot. A handle's r/Rr/R is fixed a third of the way to the neighboring point and is greyed; only its value moves, which sets the slope. G1, on a point between the root and the tip of a Cubic Bezier, keeps the curve smooth there: checking it sets both handles to the average of their slopes, and afterward moving either handle moves the other to match. Split adds a point on the curve at r/R Split; on a Cubic Bezier the new point comes with two handles and the curve keeps its shape. Del deletes the selected point, with its handles; the root and tip points cannot be deleted. Split Pick and Del Pick act on the next click in the plot: Split Pick splits the curve at the r/Rr/R clicked, and Del Pick deletes the point clicked.

Linear suits a straight taper or a linear twist. Cubic Bezier is the easiest type to shape by hand, since its handles set the slope at each point. PCHIP passes through every point, but its shape between them is hard to steer; it is the type a BEM import gives (see BEM Files). Convert to Approximate Cubic Bezier refits a single curve as a cubic Bezier with fewer points.

How a Blade Is Built

Each section is laid out with its chord in the plane of rotation, scaled to the local chord, with its Construct X/C point on the radial line at its radius. It is then twisted, offset, swept and wrapped, as Figures 5-10 show for normal rotation; in each figure the earlier state is dashed.

Rake and Skew, or Axial and Tangential, can give the same shapes, so use one pair or the other; do not mix them. Rake and Skew are perpendicular and parallel to the local chord, and so turn with the twist. Axial and Tangential are perpendicular and parallel to the plane of rotation.

Skew and Tangential move sections sideways off the radial line, so they change the blade's true diameter: a tip section whose construction point is offset by tt in the plane of rotation lies R2+t2\sqrt{R^2 + t^2} from the shaft, beyond the Diameter. Rake and Axial keep each section at its radius, so a blade that leans along a varying curve grows longer instead.

A blade whose Rake or Axial curve is not constant leans along that curve. Each section is tilted by an angle κ\kappa so that it stays perpendicular to the curve (Figure 7). κ\kappa is not a parameter: OpenVSP computes it from the slope of the Rake and Axial curves.

Rotor Metrics

The Design tab reports several rotor metrics commonly used to describe propellers and rotors. The activity factor and the integrated design lift coefficient are mostly used for propellers; the mean chords and solidities mostly for rotors. Each integral is taken over the blade curves themselves, by adaptive quadrature, not over a set of sampled stations. Below, x=r/Rx = r/R, c(x)c(x) is the Chord curve as c/Rc/R, xrx_r the root, and x0x_0 the activity factor's lower limit r_0 (0.2 by default, and never inboard of the root).

The Activity Factor and the integrated design lift coefficient CLi are

AF=10000016∫x01c2x3dx,CLi=4∫x01Cli(x)x3dx \mathrm{AF} = \frac{100000}{16}\int_{x_0}^{1}\frac{c}{2}\,x^3\,dx, \qquad C_{L_i} = 4\int_{x_0}^{1} C_{l_i}(x)\,x^3\,dx

The mean chord C/R, the thrust-weighted mean chord C_T/R and the power-weighted mean chord C_P/R are

CR=11−xr∫xr1cdx,CTR=31−xr3∫xr1cx2dx,CPR=41−xr4∫xr1cx3dx \frac{C}{R} = \frac{1}{1 - x_r}\int_{x_r}^{1} c\,dx, \qquad \frac{C_T}{R} = \frac{3}{1 - x_r^3}\int_{x_r}^{1} c\,x^2\,dx, \qquad \frac{C_P}{R} = \frac{4}{1 - x_r^4}\int_{x_r}^{1} c\,x^3\,dx

and the solidities σ\sigma, σT\sigma_T and σP\sigma_P, blade area over disk area, follow from them:

σ=NπCR,σT=NπCTR,σP=NπCPR \sigma = \frac{N}{\pi}\,\frac{C}{R}, \qquad \sigma_T = \frac{N}{\pi}\,\frac{C_T}{R}, \qquad \sigma_P = \frac{N}{\pi}\,\frac{C_P}{R}

Blade Spacing and Balance

Blade 1 is the reference blade. Each other blade ii sits at a fixed angle ϕi\phi_i ahead of blade 1, the Parm BladeAz_i, measured in the direction of rotation (ϕ1=0\phi_1 = 0). These are numbered by blade, while the per-blade angles below are numbered from blade 2 as 1: BladeAz_2 and FlapAngle_1 both belong to blade 2. The More tab sets the spacing.

Mode Spacing ϕi\phi_i
Uniform ϕi=360∘(i−1)/N\phi_i = 360^\circ (i-1)/N.
Free Set each ϕi\phi_i, or with Delta checked, each one's difference DeltaAz_i from uniform.
Balanced As Free, but the last two blades are placed so that the blades balance; with N≤3N \le 3, uniform.

X1 Balance and X2 Balance report the centroid of the blades' unit vectors as a fraction of the radius, along blade 1 and across it:

X1=1N∑i=1Ncos⁡ϕi,X2=−ϵN∑i=1Nsin⁡ϕi X_1 = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\cos\phi_i, \qquad X_2 = -\frac{\epsilon}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin\phi_i

Both are zero for a balanced rotor. X2X_2 is positive toward blade 1's trailing edge (+Z+Z for normal rotation). They treat the blades as identical, so they depend only on the spacing, not on any blade's chord or mass; the hinge angles are not considered.

Hinges

OpenVSP can model a fully articulated rotor, with a flap hinge, a lag hinge and a feather bearing on each blade, and blades that fold. Each of these motions is a turn about a hinge. A hinge at zero angle does nothing, so a rotor without, say, lag hinges simply leaves the lag angle at zero; OpenVSP does not solve for the blades' motion, so it does not need to know which hinges a rotor really has. A hinge may sit at the hub, r/R=0r/R = 0. A flap hinge there with first-harmonic flapping (see Harmonic Blade Motion) makes a two-bladed rotor teeter as one rigid body. With more blades and first-harmonic feathering as well, θ1c=β1s\theta_{1c} = \beta_{1s} and θ1s=−β1c\theta_{1s} = -\beta_{1c}, it stands in for a gimballed hub to second order in the tilt.

Each hinge hh turns the blade, and everything outboard of the hinge, by its angle γh\gamma_h, right-handed about the line through its point 𝒐h\mathbf{o}_h along its axis 𝒂̂h\hat{\mathbf{a}}_h:

Jh=T(𝒐h)R(𝒂̂h,γh)T(−𝒐h) J_h = T(\mathbf{o}_h)\; R(\hat{\mathbf{a}}_h, \gamma_h)\; T(-\mathbf{o}_h)

The hinges are applied hub to tip in order of their radial position, so each hinge carries every hinge outboard of it. Hinges at the same radius go flap, lag, fold, feather. With the default positions (flap 0.05, lag 0.10, feather 0.15, fold 0.20) the order is flap, lag, feather, fold.

Hinges are given in the blade axes BB, whose origin is the rotor center: xBx_B along the chord from the leading edge to the trailing edge, yBy_B radially out along the blade, and zBz_B toward the thrust (Figure 12). The blade axes follow the direction of rotation, so a hinge means the same on a reversed rotor, and they turn with the blade's precone, spacing and rotation.

The flap, lag and fold hinges each have a point set by Radial/R, Offset/R and Axial/R: out along the blade, along the chord toward the trailing edge, and downstream along the shaft, each as a fraction of the radius RR (Figure 13):

𝒐h=R(Off,Rad,−Ax) \mathbf{o}_h = R\,\left(\mathrm{Off},\ \mathrm{Rad},\ -\mathrm{Ax}\right)

Their axes are turned from a starting direction in two steps:

𝒂̂flap=Ry(θF)Rz(−δ3)x̂B,𝒂̂lag=Ry(θL)Rx(−α1)(−ẑB),𝒂̂fold=Ry(Az)Rz(−El)(−x̂B) \hat{\mathbf{a}}_\mathrm{flap} = R_y(\theta_F)\,R_z(-\delta_3)\,\hat{x}_B, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{a}}_\mathrm{lag} = R_y(\theta_L)\,R_x(-\alpha_1)\,(-\hat{z}_B), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{a}}_\mathrm{fold} = R_y(\mathrm{Az})\,R_z(-\mathrm{El})\,(-\hat{x}_B)

The feather bearing turns the blade about the radial line through the root's feather axis:

𝒐feather=(crFeatherOffsetXoC,RRFeatherAx,0),𝒂̂feather=ŷB \mathbf{o}_\mathrm{feather} = \left(c_r\,\mathrm{FeatherOffsetXoC},\ R\,\mathrm{RFeatherAx},\ 0\right), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{a}}_\mathrm{feather} = \hat{y}_B

  • The hinge's Azimuth (θF\theta_F, θL\theta_L, Az) turns its axis about the radial direction; it is not a pitch angle. Elevation (δ3\delta_3 for the flap, El for the fold) turns the axis in the plane of rotation; Cant (α1\alpha_1) turns the lag axis about the chord.
  • A skewed flap hinge couples pitch to flap: a turn γ\gamma about a flap hinge at δ3\delta_3 flaps the blade by about γcos⁡δ3\gamma\cos\delta_3 and changes its pitch by about −γsin⁡δ3-\gamma\sin\delta_3, so Δθ=−tan⁡δ3Δβ\Delta\theta = -\tan\delta_3\,\Delta\beta. A canted lag hinge couples pitch to lag in the same way through α1\alpha_1. The fold axis starts on −x̂B-\hat{x}_B, so a positive fold with a positive El pitches the blade up.
  • The feather axis is offset by crc_r\,FeatherOffsetXoC along xBx_B, where crc_r is the root chord. It passes through the root section's Feather Axis X/C point when the root has no Rake, Skew, Sweep, Axial or Tangential offset (Figure 9). Its Radial/R only places the feather bearing among the other hinges; sliding a bearing along its own axis does not move the blade.

Placing the Blades

Each blade ii is the blade as built, 𝒑0\mathbf{p}_0, moved by

𝒑=RX(−ϵϕi)RX(−ϵψref)RX(−ϵψ)RZ(βp)J1J2J3J4𝒑0 \mathbf{p} = R_X(-\epsilon\,\phi_i)\; R_X(-\epsilon\,\psi_\mathrm{ref})\; R_X(-\epsilon\,\psi)\; R_Z(\beta_p)\; J_1\, J_2\, J_3\, J_4\; \mathbf{p}_0

where RA(θ)R_A(\theta) is a right-handed turn by θ\theta about axis AA, and

  • J1…J4J_1 \ldots J_4 are the four hinges, sorted hub to tip by radial position (see Hinges);
  • βp\beta_p is the Precone, a turn about ZZ through the hub;
  • ψ\psi is Rotate;
  • ψref\psi_\mathrm{ref} is PsiRef (ψ\psi Ref on the Motion tab);
  • ϕi\phi_i is the blade's spacing (zero for blade 1).

The turns about XX are by minus ϵ\epsilon times the angle, so each is a turn in the direction of rotation. The product reads two equivalent ways. Right to left, each factor turns the blade about an axis fixed where it lies with everything at zero: the tip-most hinge first, the blade's spacing last. Left to right, each factor carries every factor to its right with it, so the precone tips the hinges and each hinge carries every hinge outboard of it. The Geom's placement and any symmetry act after this; see Scripting. Figures 21-23 show the steps.

Figure 21. The four hinges on the blade, each at its own point inboard of the blade root: flap, lag, feather, fold, hub to tip.

Figure 22. Precone turns the blade about ZZ through the hub.

Figure 23. Every motion: three blades with their hinges turned, precone and Rotate, and blade 1's hinge axes where the joints inboard of them carry them.

The blade's azimuth ψi\psi_i, the angle harmonic motion uses and the usual rotorcraft azimuth, is

ψi=ψ+ϕi \psi_i = \psi + \phi_i

ψ=0\psi = 0 lies along +Y+Y turned by PsiRef in the direction of rotation. PsiRef is not part of ψi\psi_i: changing it turns the whole rotor, harmonic pattern and all, while changing Rotate moves the blades through the pattern (Figures 24-25). In helicopter position, PsiRef 270 puts ψ=0\psi = 0 over the tail.

Blade Motion Modes

Mode on the Motion tab chooses how the flap, lag and feather angles are given.

Mode Angles
Collective Every blade takes the same angle, one value for each hinge.
Individual Each blade has its own angle for each hinge.
Harmonic Each blade's angle is a harmonic series in its azimuth (below).

In Individual mode, pick the blade to edit with Blade; the active blade is outlined in the 3D view. The fold is separate: its Ind. toggle gives each blade its own fold angle.

Harmonic Blade Motion

In Harmonic mode each hinge's angle on blade ii is a mean plus NhN_h harmonics of the blade's azimuth ψi\psi_i:

β(ψi)=β0+∑n=1Nh[βnccos(nψi)+βnssin(nψi)] \beta(\psi_i) = \beta_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{N_h}\left[\,\beta_{nc}\cos(n\,\psi_i) + \beta_{ns}\sin(n\,\psi_i)\,\right]

and likewise for the lag ζ\zeta and the feather θ\theta. Harmonics sets NhN_h (0 to 20).

Hinge Mean Cosine, sine coefficients
Flap FlapMean, β0\beta_0 FlapCos_n, FlapSin_n: βnc\beta_{nc}, βns\beta_{ns}
Lag LagMean, ζ0\zeta_0 LagCos_n, LagSin_n: ζnc\zeta_{nc}, ζns\zeta_{ns}
Feather FeatherMean, θ0\theta_0 FeatherCos_n, FeatherSin_n: θnc\theta_{nc}, θns\theta_{ns}

These are the angles about each hinge's own axis, and the signs follow CAMRAD II. For a flap hinge with no Elevation or Azimuth (δ3=0\delta_3 = 0, θF=0\theta_F = 0) the flap angle is the classical flap β\beta: β0\beta_0 is the coning, positive β1c\beta_{1c} raises the tip at ψ=0\psi = 0 and positive β1s\beta_{1s} at ψ=90∘\psi = 90^\circ, tilting the tip path plane. A skewed hinge also turns the blade about its chord, so the flap out of the plane is smaller and the pitch changes too (see Hinges).

In this mode the angle sliders show, greyed, blade 1's angle at the current Rotate (Figure 26). Each blade's own angle is set too, at the current Rotate, so switching to Individual starts from the harmonic shape at that rotation angle. Because each blade's angles follow its own azimuth, turning the rotor by one blade spacing carries every blade to where the next one was, with that blade's angles: the tip path stays put while the blades move along it (Figures 27-29).

Reverse Rotation

Checking Rev reverses the direction of rotation and makes the propeller the mirror image of the normal one: the sections flip chordwise, every tangential quantity changes sign (Skew, Tangential, Sweep, Twist, the Construct and Feather offsets), the blades turn the other way, and every hinge mirrors with the blade. In the equations above this is the factor ϵ=−1\epsilon = -1.

Two rotors that are mirror images in the plane Y=0Y = 0 between them differ in two parameters: the second has Rev checked and PsiRef 180 degrees more. Every other parameter, the hinge and harmonic angles included, keeps its value, and each blade mirrors the same-numbered blade of the other rotor (Figures 30-31). The 180 degrees are needed because the mirror moves ψ=0\psi = 0 to the other side of the shaft; in helicopter position, PsiRef 270 for the normal rotor and 90 for the reversed one both put ψ=0\psi = 0 over the tail. With PsiRef unchanged, the reversed rotor is instead the mirror image in the plane through the shaft and the propeller's YY axis.

Figure 30. Side-by-side rotors in helicopter position, normal (grey) and Rev with PsiRef 180 degrees more (tan): mirror images in Y=0Y = 0, turning opposite ways.

Figure 31. The same rotors: each blade mirrors the same-numbered blade, and both have ψ=0\psi = 0 over the tail.

A hinge axis does not simply mirror with its arrow. A positive turn is right-handed about its axis, and the mirror turns the curl around, so the reversed rotor's axis points opposite to the mirror image of the normal one's arrow (Figure 32).

Clones of a Propeller

A Clone of a propeller has Rotor Rotation under Copy From Original. Checked, the default, the Clone's blades follow the original's Rotate. Unchecked, the Clone has its own Rotate in the Rotor group, and its blades, with their harmonic angles, are placed at that angle. Rotors of one design turned apart in phase, such as intermeshing or tandem rotors, are Clones with their own Rotate. A counter-rotating rotor also needs the opposite hand. A Clone cannot be reversed apart from its original, so flip it instead (XForm tab, Flip) in a plane that contains the shaft.

Scripting

The OpenVSP API has functions specific to propellers, both for modeling a propeller and for querying how its blades move.

For modeling, a script sets up a propeller from a preset, edits its blade curves, and reads and writes BEM files:

Call Purpose

A script finds a parm by name and group. The propeller's design parms, the fold, Feather and the blade spacing are in the Design group; the flap, lag and feather bearing, PsiRef, the motion mode, the harmonics and the per-blade flap, lag and feather angles (Feather_n too) are in Articulation. Older Files

Models saved before OpenVSP 3.56 open as they were saved, and some propellers change shape:

  • On a propeller with reverse rotation, Feather Offset and the fold axis's Offset/R, Azimuth and Elevation now mirror with the rotation, as the rest of the blade does. Change their signs to keep the earlier blades.
  • A blade now feathers at its feather bearing, Radial/R 0.15, where it used to feather outboard of every hinge. A propeller with both a fold and a feather angle, folding outboard of the bearing, now folds about a feathered hinge. Set the feather bearing's Radial/R to 1.0 to keep the earlier blades.

BEM Files

A BEM (blade element) file holds the generic information a blade element analysis needs: the rotor's diameter, blade count, pitch, precone, center and axis, the blade curves at a set of stations, and a section shape at each station. It is not written for any one blade element program; a program that reads it takes what it needs.

Exporting a propeller to a BEM file samples the curves at Num_U stations from root to tip, spaced by the root and tip clustering, and writes each section as Num_W points around an untwisted section of unit chord. The default propeller gives, shortened:

Importing a BEM file makes a new propeller. It takes the diameter, blade count, Beta 3/4 (Feather then follows from it and the twist), precone and placement from the file, and every curve, as a PCHIP spline, through the file's stations. It keeps only a root and a tip section, both NACA 16-series; the section shapes in the file are not read.

A BEM file often lists many stations, many more than OpenVSP's smooth blade curves need, and each becomes a point on every curve, which slows every update. Approximate All on the Blade tab (ApproximateAllPropellerPCurves in the API) refits each curve as a cubic Bezier with far fewer points, which is easier to edit: a file with 41 stations gives a Chord curve of 16 points, a Twist curve of 4, and Thick and CLi curves of 10.
