XForm Tab

Contents

Every component (Geom) in OpenVSP is edited in a window whose first tabs are the same for all of them: Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub. The XForm tab places the Geom in the model: where it is and how it is turned, what it is attached to, whether it is reflected, mirrored or repeated, and its overall size. Every Geom is built in its own axes and then placed by these settings, which How the Placement Is Built puts together.

Quick Start

A part that belongs to another part, such as a wing on a fuselage or a nacelle under a wing, is made a child of it and attached to it, so that it moves with it. In the Geom Browser, select the parent before you click Add: the new Geom becomes its child.

A wing on a fuselage

  1. Select the fuselage and add a Wing.
  2. On the XForm tab, set Attach To Parent to Translate Comp and Rotate Comp.
  3. Place the wing with XLoc, YLoc and ZLoc, now measured from the fuselage's origin in the fuselage's axes.
  4. Leave Symmetry at its default, Planar XZ about the wing's attachment: the left wing is the mirror image of the right one in the fuselage's center plane, wherever the fuselage is moved.

A nacelle under a wing

  1. Select the wing and add a Pod.
  2. Set Translate to η\eta MN and Rotate to Comp, so that the nacelle sits at a fraction of the span and keeps the wing's axes.
  3. Set η\eta to the spanwise station and M 1 for the leading edge (where N has no effect), then move the nacelle forward and down with XLoc and ZLoc, now measured from that point in the wing's axes.
  4. To repeat it on the other wing, set Symmetry About to the wing (2 in the list), keep Attach, and check Planar XZ: the nacelle is mirrored in the same plane as the wing (see Symmetry).

Location and Rotation

XLoc, YLoc and ZLoc move the Geom; XRot, YRot and ZRot turn it, in degrees. The rotations are applied in the order ZRot, YRot, XRot, each about an axis that stays fixed: the shape is first turned about ZZ, the result about YY, and that about XX. The same turn is reached by turning about the Geom's own axes in the order X, Y, Z, each turn carrying the axes for the next.

The Geom turns about its Rot Origin(X): a point on its own XX axis, given as a fraction of its length. For a Fuselage, Pod, Cobra, Ellipsoid or BOR this is the fraction of its length along XX from the nose, for a Wing of the root chord of its first section; for Stack, Propeller and the Geoms without a length along XX it has no effect. The rotation origin is also the point the location refers to: XLoc, YLoc, ZLoc in Abs give where the rotation origin lies in the model, while in Rel they give how far it has been moved. A 10-unit pod with Rot Origin 0.5 and nothing else changed shows an absolute XLoc of 5 and a relative one of 0.

Placing a Part Against Others

To put a part a set distance from the rest of the model, such as a battery or a tank packed inside a fuselage, use Snap To in the Model menu (Figure 1) rather than adjusting by eye. Set the Target Min Dist and the Set of components to keep clear of. Then either hold Alt (Option) while dragging one of the part's sliders, XLoc for example, and the part stops at that clearance instead of passing into the others; or pick a parameter in Manual Collision Detection and click Increase or Decrease to move it until the clearance is reached. Check Clearance reports the present minimum distance.

Abs and Rel

The location and rotation are kept two ways, absolute (Abs: in the model's axes) and relative (Rel: in the axes the Geom is attached to, see Attach to Parent). The Coord System buttons choose which of the two the sliders show and set; the other is computed from it. A Geom that is not attached to anything has the same absolute and relative values, apart from the rotation origin's effect above.

The choice does not change what happens when the parent moves. A child always keeps its relative placement when its parent moves, so an attached child moves with its parent even while its sliders show absolute values; Abs is a way to put a child at a known point in the model, not a way to pin it there.

The choice does matter when the child's own attachment changes. In Rel, the child keeps its relative values and jumps to the same relative place in the new axes. In Abs, it keeps its place in the model and its relative values are recomputed for the new attachment. To attach a part that is already where it belongs, switch to Abs, change the attachment, and switch back to Rel: the part does not move, and from then on it follows its parent.

Attach to Parent

A child Geom may be attached to its parent. Attachment chooses the axes its relative location and rotation are measured in, separately for the two: Translate chooses the origin of those axes and Rotate their directions (Figure 2). With both at None, the default, the child is placed in the model's axes and stays where it is when the parent moves.

Figure 2. The Attach To Parent group of a Pod whose parent is the default Wing, attached by Translate η\eta MN and Rotate Comp at η\eta 0.4, M 1 and N 0. The rows of the coordinates not chosen are greyed and show where the same point lies in them.

Translate and Rotate are independent: Translate Comp with Rotate None follows the parent's moves but not its turns, which keeps a part level however the parent is turned (Figure 3).

Figure 3. A fuselage moved and turned by ZRot 20, with a child pod at the same relative location (12, -5, 0) attached three ways. Each dashed arrow is that location, measured from the origin and along the axes the attachment chooses: the model's (XX, YY) or the fuselage's (XpX_p, YpY_p).

Changing the attachment moves the child or not depending on Abs and Rel (see Abs and Rel). The attachment is computed on the parent's first surface, not on its symmetric copies. For a parent without a surface, such as a Blank, UW and the others act as Comp. A child of a Hinge is always carried by the hinge and its attachment controls are greyed (see Hinge); a child of a Routing Geom cannot be attached.

While a Geom is selected, the 3D view draws the frame it is attached in as dashed axes (red XX, green YY, blue ZZ), at the point its relative location is measured from. With attachment None that is the model's origin and axes. In the Geom Browser, the line joining an attached child to its parent is drawn thick, and the lines from a Hinge to its children are double. A Geom whose attachment is set but which has no parent, for example after it was moved out from under its parent, has its name drawn lighter; it is placed as if attached to nothing.

Attachment is the way to keep a model together as it changes. Attach each part to the part it is mounted on, so that moving or resizing a fuselage carries its wing, and the wing its nacelles; an unattached part stays behind and has to be moved by hand every time. Where an assembly should not hang off one of its parts, attach it to a Blank and move the Blank.

Attachment Coordinates

UW is a point on the parent's surface. U runs along the parent from 0 at its start to 1 at its end, and W around each section from 0 to 1. With 0N instead of 01, U counts sections, with each cross section at a whole number; on a Wing the root and tip caps count as sections, so a one-panel wing runs U = 0 to 3 and its panel lies between U = 1 and 2. Where W starts depends on the Geom: on a Wing, W = 0 is the trailing edge, 0.25 the lower surface, 0.5 the leading edge and 0.75 the upper surface; on a Fuselage W = 0 is the +Y+Y side and W runs down, round the bottom; on a Pod W = 0 is the top and W runs toward +Y+Y. The UW axes are XX along the surface in the direction of increasing U, ZZ the outward normal, and YY completing the set (Figure 4).

Figure 4. UW attachment axes at two points of a fuselage. zz is the outward normal and xx points along increasing U; the dashed lines are the sections at those U.

RST is a point inside the parent. R is the same as U. At that station, S picks a pair of points on the surface, one on each half of the section: at W = S/2 and W = 1 - S/2. On a wing, S = 0 is the trailing edge and S = 1 the leading edge. T places the point on the straight line between them, from 0 on the first half (a wing's lower surface) to 1 on the second (its upper surface); T = 0.5 is midway. So T = 0 or 1 lies on the surface, and values between lie inside (Figure 5).

Figure 5. RST at the root section of a wing of 20% thickness. Each S picks a point on each surface (dots, joined by dashed lines); T runs along that line from the lower surface to the upper.

LMN is RST measured by distance. L is the fraction of the parent's length along its center line (0D gives it as a length instead), where R, like U, counts the parent's sections whatever their lengths. M is the fraction of the distance from the S = 0 edge to the S = 1 edge, along the line midway between the two halves; on a wing it is the fraction of the chord from the trailing edge, so M = 0.25 is at 75% chord. N is the same as T. Use LMN to place something at a fraction of a length or a chord.

The difference between U (or R) and L is large when the cross sections are unevenly spaced (Figure 6). Each span between neighboring cross sections takes an equal share of U, however long it is: on a fuselage of five cross sections, U = 0.25 is always the second and U = 0.5 the third, wherever they have been placed, while L = 0.5 is always half way along. Choose by what the part should stay with when the parent is reshaped: U or R to stay at a cross section, such as a wing at a fuselage frame; L to stay at a fraction of the length.

Figure 6. A fuselage 30 long with its cross sections at 0, 3, 7.5, 22.5 and 30. Above, the points U = 0 to 1 on its center line fall at the cross sections; below, the points L = 0 to 1 are evenly spaced along it.

η\eta MN is offered when the parent is a Wing (or a Clone of one). η\eta is the fraction of the wing's span from root to tip, measured over its sections without the caps, and M and N are as in LMN; see Spanwise Coordinate Eta. On a wing, η\eta is the spanwise choice that corresponds to L: U and R count the wing's sections and caps, so a part placed by U stays with a section when a panel's span is changed, while a part placed by η\eta stays at its fraction of the span. Use η\eta MN for nacelles, pylons and stores placed at a fraction of the semispan.

The RST, LMN and η\eta MN axes are XX along R (along the parent), ZZ normal to the R and S directions, and YY completing the set.

UW, RST and LMN describe one point, and while one of them is in use OpenVSP keeps the others up to date. Switching from one to another therefore leaves the attachment point where it was.

Flip

Flip reflects the Geom's shape in the chosen planes of its own axes, in place: it makes the mirror image of the Geom, not a second copy. Use it for one opposite-handed part made from the same definition, such as a single left-hand nacelle or a counter-rotating Clone of a propeller (see Propeller). It is not offered for Blank and Routing Geoms, which have no shape to reflect, nor for Auxiliary and Conformal Geoms, which take their parent's flip.

The reflection happens in the Geom's own axes, before it is turned and moved. A wing flipped in its XZ plane becomes a left wing; turned by XRot 30 as well, that left wing tips down, not up, because the turn acts on the reflected shape. A left wing made by Planar XZ symmetry is the mirror image of the turned right wing, so it tips up (Figure 7).

Figure 7. The default Wing, without its symmetry, turned by XRot 30 (top), flipped in its XZ plane and turned by XRot 30 (middle), and with Planar XZ symmetry and XRot 30 (bottom), seen from the front. The dashed line is the model's YY axis.

A flip changes the shape, not the Geom's axes. Children attached by Comp follow the parent's unreflected axes; children attached by UW, RST, LMN or η\eta MN follow the reflected surface. Two planes of flip together amount to a half turn, not a reflection.

Symmetry

Symmetry adds copies of the Geom. Each Planar plane doubles the number of copies by reflecting all of them in it. Axial makes NN copies, each turned 360/N360/N degrees from the last about the chosen axis. Planar symmetry is applied first and axial symmetry to its result, so XZ with Axial X and N=3N = 3 gives six copies. The copies are part of the Geom: they carry its sub-surfaces and its specified mass, and they appear in every analysis, but its children are not copied; give each child its own symmetry.

A new Wing has Planar XZ symmetry. When a wing is turned XRot 90 to make a vertical tail on the center plane, its copy lands on top of it. Coincident surfaces trouble meshing and the analyses: CFD Mesh leaves the tail and its copy out of the mesh altogether, with a warning in its console. The wing's total span also counts both. Uncheck XZ for a single vertical tail; keep it only for twin tails moved off the center plane.

The planes and the axis belong to a frame chosen by About and the two buttons beside it (Figure 8). The list holds the global origin (0), the Geom itself (1), its parent (2), its grandparent (3), and so on.

Figure 8. The Symmetry group of the same Pod: About its parent, 2 WingGeom, with Attach, and Planar XZ.

  • Attach uses the frame that Geom is attached in: its parent's origin and axes, or the surface point, if it is attached; the model's origin if it is not.
  • Object uses that Geom's own origin and axes, where it has been placed.

The default, the Geom itself with Attach, mirrors a Geom in the frame it is attached in. For a wing attached to its fuselage by Comp, that is the fuselage's center plane, wherever the fuselage is (Figure 9). When the attachment is a point on the surface (UW, RST, LMN or η\eta MN), the frame sits at that point, so the default mirrors the wing about a plane through the attachment point instead of the fuselage's center: choose the fuselage in the list, with Object. To mirror in the model's own planes, choose 0, the global origin.

Figure 9. A fuselage moved and turned by ZRot 10, with a wing attached by Translate and Rotate Comp and Planar XZ symmetry. Top: the default, about the wing's attachment, mirrors it in the fuselage's center plane. Bottom: about the global origin, it is mirrored in the model's XZ plane.

How the Placement Is Built

A point 𝒑\mathbf{p} of the Geom as it is built in its own axes is placed in the model at

𝒙k=QSkQ−1AT(𝒕)T(𝒄)RX(θX)RY(θY)RZ(θZ)T(−𝒄)F𝒑 \mathbf{x}_k = Q\,S_k\,Q^{-1}\; A\; T(\mathbf{t})\; T(\mathbf{c})\, R_X(\theta_X)\, R_Y(\theta_Y)\, R_Z(\theta_Z)\, T(-\mathbf{c})\; F\;\mathbf{p}

read right to left:

  • FF is the flip, a reflection in each checked plane of the Geom's own axes;
  • 𝒄\mathbf{c} is the rotation origin, T(⋅)T(\cdot) a move, and RX(θX)R_X(\theta_X) etc. the rotations by XRot, YRot, ZRot;
  • 𝒕\mathbf{t} is the relative location;
  • AA is the attachment: the parent's frame chosen by Translate and Rotate (A=IA = I for None);
  • QQ is the symmetry frame chosen by About, and SkS_k the kk-th symmetric copy, a combination of the planar reflections and axial turns (S0=IS_0 = I, the Geom itself).

The absolute location and rotation are the move and turn of AT(𝒕)T(𝒄)RT(−𝒄)A\,T(\mathbf{t})\,T(\mathbf{c})\,R\,T(-\mathbf{c}) expressed in the model's axes; children attached by Comp hang off this same product, without FF.

Scale

Scale multiplies the Geom's dimensions (lengths, diameters, chords, spans, and sub-surface and CFD source sizes given as lengths), keeping its proportions; the shape grows about the Geom's own origin. It does not move the Geom, and it does not scale its children or their placement.

The factor is relative to the size when it was last accepted. Setting it back to 1, or Reset, returns the Geom to that size; Accept makes the current size the new size at 1. An edit made while scaled is scaled back with everything else: a pod scaled by 2 whose length is then set to 25 is 12.5 long when the scale returns to 1. So accept a scale before editing the scaled Geom.

Scripting

Every control on this tab except the Scale's Reset and Accept buttons is a Parm, set and read with SetParmVal and GetParmVal by name and group; GetParmActiveFlag tells whether a control is active or greyed, as of the last Update. Reset is ResetGeomScale and Accept is AcceptGeomScale. The parent a Geom is attached to is set with SetGeomParent (or chosen when the Geom is added with AddGeom) and read with GetGeomParent.

To set a child's absolute location or rotation from a script, call UpdateGeom on the child right after setting it, or set it with SetParmValUpdate, which sets the Parm and updates the Geom in one call. The child then takes its relative values from the new absolute ones. Update alone updates the parent first, which places the child from its relative values, so the new absolute ones are replaced.

The attachment coordinates can be evaluated and converted without attaching anything: CompPnt01 gives the point at U, W and CompPntRST the point at R, S, T; ProjPnt01 and FindRST find the coordinates of a point; ConvertRSTtoLMN and ConvertLMNtoRST convert between volume coordinates, and ConvertUtoEta and ConvertEtatoU between U and η\eta on a wing. All but the last two have vector forms (CompVecPnt01, FindRSTVec, ...) for many points at once.
