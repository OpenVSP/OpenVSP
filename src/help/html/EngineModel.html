Engine Model

Contents

A Fuselage or a Stack can stand for an engine nacelle, an inlet, an exhaust or the flowpath inside them. The engine model lets the same sections be handed to analyses in different ways -- as an open flow-through nacelle, as a solid with the flowpath removed, closed at the lips or at the faces, or stretched upstream and downstream to the edge of a CFD domain -- without rebuilding them. It works in two steps. The Engine Definition says what the sections describe and where the inlet and outlet lips and faces are; the Engine Representation says what is built from them. Both are groups on the Design tab of the Fuselage and the Stack.

Engine modeling has many choices, and not every combination of them makes a valid or useful model. The quickest way to a working one is a Stack preset: each nacelle preset lays out the sections and sets the whole engine model to match (see Starting from a Preset). The Stack Presets page draws every one of them.

Quick Start

  1. Add a Stack and set Stack Preset to one of the Nacelle Flowthrough presets. The preset lays out the sections and fills in the Engine Definition.
  2. Place it with the XForm tab, and size its sections on the XSec tab.
  3. The lips and faces are drawn on the nacelle while Show Stations is checked, as it is by default.
  4. The preset comes with Inlet Mode FLOWTHROUGH, an open nacelle. Choose FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE under Engine Representation for a solid nacelle and a flowpath that cuts through whatever it meets.

Starting from a Preset

Stack Preset on the Stack's Design tab replaces the Stack's sections with a nacelle and sets its XSec Order, its end caps, and the parts of its Engine Definition, stations and Engine Representation that the preset's definition uses, with the simplest representation of it. The controls it does not use keep their values, and so do the Extension settings. Pick the preset by what the sections should describe; the representation can then be changed freely.

A Fuselage has no presets. Its engine model is set by hand, with the same controls.

Engine Definition

Geom Type says which parts of an engine the sections describe: NONE (an ordinary body, the default), INLET, BOTH or OUTLET. The other controls are used only when it is not NONE.

Inlet Type and Outlet Type say how the sections describe each end.

Type The sections describe
FLOWTHROUGH The whole nacelle wall in one loop: the outside, both lips and the flowpath inside. An Inlet Type, for Geom Type BOTH; the menu also offers it with INLET, where it does not give a flow-through nacelle.
FLOWPATH Only the duct inside, from the lip to the face.
INLET TO LIP, OUTLET TO LIP The outside only, ending at the lip.
INLET TO FACE, OUTLET TO FACE The outside, around the lip and into the duct, ending at the face.

With Geom Type BOTH and an Inlet Type of FLOWTHROUGH or FLOWPATH, the outlet is described the same way, and Outlet Type is greyed.

The lip is where the outside surface meets the duct; the face is the end of the duct inside the nacelle, where the compressor face or the turbine exit would be. Figure 1 shows the curve the sections trace for each type, and where its lips and faces lie.

The inlet is at the left. Dots are the sections; grey, the flat ends that point sections close; dotted, the stations the definition uses.

Stations

The four stations mark the lips and faces on the sections: Inlet Lip, Inlet Face, Outlet Face and Outlet Lip. Each is set either by the index of a section (Indx) or by U, a fraction from 0 at the first section to 1 at the last, chosen by the small toggle before each (Figure 2). Only the stations the definition needs are active: the lip of each end described, and its face unless its type is TO LIP. A FLOWTHROUGH nacelle needs all four.

Figure 2. The Engine Definition and Engine Representation of the Nacelle Flowthrough Mid Origin preset, with the Inlet Face given by U. Outlet Type is greyed because the inlet is FLOWTHROUGH, and Outlet Mode because the Inlet Mode is.

Show Stations draws the active stations on the component in the 3D view, as heavy dashed lines in the colors of their labels: the inlet lip cyan, the inlet face magenta, the outlet face green, the outlet lip yellow (Figure 3). They are drawn only while the component is in the Shown set and its feature lines are shown.

Figure 3. The stations on the Nacelle Flowthrough Mid Origin preset, cut through its axis, with the section numbers. The inlet is at the left.

Engine Representation

Inlet Mode and Outlet Mode say what is built from the sections at each end. The result may be several surfaces: positive ones, which are solid like any other component, and negative ones, which CompGeom, CFD Mesh and the other analyses that understand negative volumes subtract from whatever they overlap. A negative flowpath cut through a nacelle that is closed at its lips leaves the same hollow nacelle as the flow-through loop, but as closed volumes, and it cuts through any wing or fuselage the nacelle is buried in. Each surface is closed where it is cut, by a cap at the lip or face, flat where the section is flat; where a negative surface ends on a positive one, the center of its cap is drawn out a hair (1/10000 of the component's size) so that the two do not share a face.

On a FLOWTHROUGH nacelle the Inlet Mode chooses between the flow-through modes, which treat both ends at once (Figure 4), and the modes for each end, of which the Inlet Mode sets the inlet and the Outlet Mode the outlet (Figure 5). Outlet Mode is greyed while a flow-through mode is chosen; the Flowthrough presets set it to TO LIP.

Mode Built
FLOWTHROUGH The loop as it is: an open nacelle, the flow passing through it.
FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE The nacelle closed at both lips, and a negative flowpath from lip to lip.
FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE ONLY The negative flowpath alone; the nacelle is not built.
TO LIP The end closed at the lip.
TO FACE The end closed at the face, leaving the inlet or outlet open as a recess.
TO FACE NEGATIVE The end closed at the lip, and a negative recess from the lip to the face.
TO FACE NEGATIVE ONLY The negative recess alone: no positive surface is built, at either end.
EXTEND The end closed at the lip and stretched upstream (inlet) or downstream (outlet); see Extension.

Figure 4. The flow-through modes on the nacelle of Figure 3, cut through its axis. Positive surfaces are grey, negative ones red and hatched.

Figure 5. The modes of each end on the same nacelle, each set as the Inlet Mode and shown at the inlet (left), with the Outlet Mode TO LIP, which closes the outlet at its lip except under TO FACE NEGATIVE ONLY, where only the recess is built. EXTEND has a Distance of 1.

Which modes are offered depends on the definition: the flow-through modes for a FLOWTHROUGH nacelle, and FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE and NEGATIVE ONLY also for a FLOWPATH with both ends; the modes that end at a face only when there is a face, except that a FLOWPATH, which has no outside to leave open, does not offer TO FACE. An outside that ends at its lip (TO LIP) can be closed at the lip or extended; one that goes on to the face (TO FACE) can use every mode but the flow-through ones. Each NEGATIVE ONLY mode is offered where its NEGATIVE mode is.

While Geom Type is not NONE, the Gen tab's Negative Volume box has no effect, though it can still be checked; the representation decides which surfaces are negative.

Representing a Flowpath

A FLOWPATH definition describes the duct itself, and the representation then says whether the duct is solid or is cut away. The modes build different surfaces from a duct than from a nacelle wall.

Definition Mode Built
Inlet or outlet TO LIP The duct as its sections draw it, positive.
Inlet or outlet TO FACE NEGATIVE The duct, negative.
Inlet or outlet TO FACE NEGATIVE ONLY Nothing.
Inlet or outlet EXTEND The duct, positive, stretched upstream (inlet) or downstream (outlet).
BOTH, Inlet Mode FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE The whole duct, negative. The Outlet Mode then closes or extends the outlet end.
BOTH, Inlet Mode FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE ONLY The whole duct, negative, as FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE.
BOTH, Inlet Mode TO FACE NEGATIVE The duct from the inlet lip to the face negative, the rest positive.
BOTH, Inlet Mode TO FACE NEGATIVE ONLY The duct from the inlet lip to the face, negative; no positive part.
BOTH, Inlet Mode TO LIP The whole duct, positive.
BOTH, Inlet Mode EXTEND The whole duct, positive, stretched upstream.

With both ends, the Outlet Mode treats the outlet end the same way: TO LIP closes it, EXTEND stretches it downstream, and TO FACE NEGATIVE makes the part from the outlet face to the outlet lip negative.

To cut an inlet duct out of a fuselage it is buried in, use TO FACE NEGATIVE for an inlet or outlet alone, and FLOWTHROUGH NEGATIVE for a duct with both ends. The three Nacelle Flowpath presets come set to TO LIP, the duct as drawn, so choose the negative mode after Set.

Extension

EXTEND stretches the lip section into a straight duct of the same section, so the flow entering an inlet or leaving an outlet can be carried to the edge of a CFD domain rather than meeting a closed face.

  • Distance is the length added.
  • Auto sets the distance to the length in XX of the components in the chosen Set (Shown by default), so the extension grows with the model. The extensions themselves are left out of that length.
  • Align X extends along the model's XX axis, the usual freestream direction, rather than along the component's own XX axis. It matters only for a component that is turned.

The inlet is extended toward −X-X, the outlet toward +X+X.
