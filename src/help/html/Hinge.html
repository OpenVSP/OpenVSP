Hinge

Contents

The Hinge component is a joint. It has no shape of its own; it is a frame that turns about, and slides along, one of its own axes, and it carries every component attached to it. Control surfaces, doors, folding wing tips, tilting nacelles and canopies are modeled by hanging the moving part off a Hinge and setting the Hinge's deflection.

Quick Start

A folding wing tip

  1. Add a Hinge. Place it on the fold line with the XForm tab, attached to the wing if it should follow the wing.
  2. Turn it so that its XX axis lies along the fold line: with the XForm rotations, or on the Orient tab (see Orienting the Hinge).
  3. Select the Hinge and add the outer wing panel, so that the panel is the Hinge's child.
  4. Set Rotate on the Motion tab.

A sliding canopy

  1. Add a Hinge. Place it at the canopy with the XForm tab, attached to the fuselage.
  2. Turn it so that its XX axis lies along the direction the canopy slides.
  3. Select the Hinge and add the canopy, so that the canopy is the Hinge's child.
  4. On the Motion tab, allow Translate, disallow Rotate, and set Translate.

How a Hinge Moves Its Children

A Hinge has two frames, both drawn in the 3D view at its origin: its own frame, placed by the XForm tab and drawn with solid axes, and the joint frame, drawn dashed, which is its own frame moved by the deflection. The joint frame is its own frame slid by Translate along the hinge axis and turned by Rotate about it, right-handed: a positive Rotate about XX turns +Y+Y toward +Z+Z (Figure 1). The hinge axis is the Hinge's own XX unless the Orient tab chooses YY or ZZ.

Figure 1. A wing panel hung off a Hinge whose axis is its XX, at Rotate 0 (dashed outline) and Rotate 40. The Hinge's own axes are solid; the joint frame, which carries the panel, is dashed.

Every child of a Hinge is attached rigidly to the joint frame. The child's Attach To Parent choices are greyed, and its relative location and rotation on the XForm tab are measured in the joint frame, so the child keeps its place on the moving part as the Hinge deflects. Its own children follow it as usual, so a whole assembly can ride one Hinge, and a Hinge can hang off another Hinge to make a chain of joints. In the Geom Browser, the lines from a Hinge to its children are drawn double, to show that they are carried by the joint (Figure 2).

Figure 2. A Hinge on a wing in the Geom Browser, carrying a flap: the line from the Hinge to its child is double.

In a chain, each Hinge's deflection is measured on the part that carries it, so the joints compose the way the real mechanism does. An aileron on a folding wing tip is a Hinge, attached to the outer panel along its trailing edge, that carries the aileron; the fold Hinge carries the outer panel, and with it the aileron Hinge. Folding the tip then carries the aileron and its hinge line with it, and the aileron deflects about its folded hinge line. Build a mechanism this way, one Hinge per real joint, each a child of the part it is mounted on; the parts then stay together at any combination of deflections (Figure 3).

Figure 3. A folding wing tip whose fold Hinge, along XX, carries the outer panel, and an aileron Hinge, along the outer panel's trailing edge, that carries the aileron. Dashed: Fold 0 and Aileron 0. Solid: Fold 60 and Aileron -20, which raises the aileron's trailing edge. Below, enlarged: a section of the folded tip seen outboard along the aileron's folded hinge line, with the aileron at 0 dotted.

Place the child where it should turn about the hinge axis: the axis passes through the Hinge's origin, so a child built with its own hinge line at its origin is placed at the Hinge's origin with zero relative location.

Motion and Limits

A motion acts only while the check box beside its slider allows it; a motion that is not allowed is held at zero (Figure 4). Rotate is allowed and Translate is not, by default. A Min or Max whose check box is checked is a limit the deflection cannot pass. To put a limit at a stop, pose the part there and click that limit's Set, which copies the current deflection into it. Rng narrows or expands the slider to the checked limits, for easier control between them.

Figure 4. The Motion tab with Translate allowed and Rotate limited to −30∘-30^\circ and 60∘60^\circ, at Rotate 20∘20^\circ. The Translate limits are not checked, so their sliders and Set buttons are greyed, and so is Translate's Rng.

Translate is a length in model units, along the hinge axis. Rotate is in degrees, between −360-360 and 360360.

The 3D view shows which motions are allowed: a circular arrow about the axis for Rotate, an arrowhead on the axis for Translate.

Orienting the Hinge

The Orient tab chooses how the Hinge's axes are set.

  • Rotations (Use XForm Tab), the default, takes them from the XForm rotations, like any other component.
  • Vectors builds them from directions. The XForm rotations are then computed from those directions and greyed.

Axis chooses which of the Hinge's own axes, XX, YY or ZZ, is the hinge axis, in either mode; the joint turns about and slides along it. With Vectors (Figure 5), Type sets the direction of that axis:

Type The hinge axis points
VECTOR3D Along the Direction Vector.
POINT3D From the Hinge's origin toward the point Offset.
SURFPT From the Hinge's origin toward the point of the parent's surface at U, W, moved by Offset.
UDIR Along the parent's surface in its uu direction at U, W.
WDIR Along the parent's surface in its ww direction at U, W.
NDIR Along the parent's surface normal at U, W.

Rel gives a vector or an offset in the axes the Hinge is attached to (the parent's, when the Hinge is attached to its parent), except SURFPT's offset, which is always in the parent's axes; Abs gives it in the model's axes. The surface types need the Hinge to have a parent with a surface. UDIR on a wing, at the station of a control surface, gives a hinge axis that follows the wing's span there.

Figure 5. The Orient tab of a Hinge on a wing, oriented by Vectors with Type SURFPT at U 0.6, W 0.5. The Offset and U, W are live; the Direction Vector is greyed and shows the hinge axis that results. Axis and Reference cannot be the same axis, so Y is greyed under Axis and X under Reference.

An axis alone allows a Hinge to rotate, but does not specify where zero rotation is. The Reference Vector, along the model's or the attachment's XX, YY or ZZ, fixes that roll: the Hinge's axis chosen as Reference is set in the plane of the hinge axis and the reference vector, square to the hinge axis (Figure 6). The reference vector is used with every Type. Its Rel and Abs choose the attachment's axes and the model's, as for the direction vector; the default is Rel.

Figure 6. A Hinge on the swept hinge line of a left wing, oriented by Vectors: Type VECTOR3D along the hinge line, Axis XX, Reference YY and the reference vector along the model's ZZ. The reference vector is turned about the Hinge's ZZ until it is square to the hinge axis, and becomes the Hinge's YY. The model's axes are drawn at its origin; the axes at the Hinge show directions only.

Flip

Flip on the XForm tab makes the Hinge move as the mirror image of its unflipped self, through each plane chosen. The Hinge's frame itself is not reflected, nor are its children: a plane that contains the hinge axis reverses the sense of Rotate, and a plane square to the axis reverses the direction of Translate. To mirror a hinged part, flip the part as well as its Hinge.

A Hinge has no symmetry of its own; the symmetry controls are greyed. A pair of mirrored control surfaces is two Hinges, or a Hinge and a Clone of it.

Other Uses of a Hinge

  • A Clone of a Hinge is a joint too. Its own children ride on it, and it can take the original's deflection or have its own.
  • The Swept Volume analysis can take a Hinge whose Translate is allowed as its secondary geometry. It then sweeps everything attached to the Hinge along the hinge axis, over the range the analysis chooses: from Min to Max, from Min to the current Translate, from the current Translate to Max, or out past the model forward or back. The Min and Max values are used whether or not their check boxes are on.

When Not to Use Hinge

  • A Gear bogie has its own retraction mechanism; describe landing gear retraction there, not with a Hinge.
  • A Propeller has its own mechanism for articulation and folding. Describe the rotation of a propeller, and movement within it (feather, flap, lag and folding), there, not with a Hinge.

Shared Tabs

The Gen and XForm tabs are common to every component; see Gen and XForm. A Hinge has neither a Mass tab nor a Sub tab, since it has no mass or surface.

Scripting

A script adds a Hinge with AddGeom( "HINGE" ). Its parms are in the group Hinge:

Parm Meaning
JointRotate, JointTranslate The deflection: degrees, and model units along the axis.
JointRotateFlag, JointTranslateFlag Whether each motion is allowed.
JointRotMin, JointRotMax, JointTransMin, JointTransMax The limits.
JointRotMinFlag, JointRotMaxFlag, JointTransMinFlag, JointTransMaxFlag Whether each limit is used.
OrientRotFlag HINGE_ORIENT_ROT (Rotations) or HINGE_ORIENT_VEC (Vectors).
PrimaryDir, SecondaryDir Axis and Reference: X_DIR, Y_DIR or Z_DIR.
PrimType HINGE_PRIM_VECTOR3D, _POINT3D, _SURFPT, _UDIR, _WDIR, _NDIR.
PrimXVec, PrimYVec, PrimZVec, and ...Rel The direction vector, Abs and Rel.
PrimXOff, PrimYOff, PrimZOff, and ...Rel The offset, Abs and Rel.
PrimVecAbsRelFlag, PrimOffAbsRelFlag, SecVecAbsRelFlag ABS or REL.
PrimULoc, PrimWLoc U and W on the parent's surface.
SecondaryVecDir The reference vector: X_DIR, Y_DIR or Z_DIR.
