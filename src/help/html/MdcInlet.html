MDC_INLET

Contents

The MDC_INLET is a set of identical inlets on a body, laid out the way DATCOM lays them out with its INLET namelist: a top-mounted or side-mounted two-dimensional inlet, or an axisymmetric one, with a ramp or a cover, and a diverter that lifts it off the body. It models the outside of an inlet: its ramp, its cowl and the way it fairs into the body behind it. No duct is modeled. DATCOM estimates an inlet's normal force, side force, moments and axial force from these inputs.

The controls carry the names and meanings of the namelist's inputs, so an inlet can be entered as DATCOM has it. The inlets stand on a body: an MDC_INLET that is a child of an MDC_BOD takes the body's radius from it, and any other is told the radius (see Placing an Inlet). The datcom2vsp.py script, in the examples, reads a DATCOM input file and builds a model with an MDC_INLET from its INLET namelist.

The MDC_INLET has one tab of its own, Design. The Gen, XForm, Mass and Sub tabs are described on their own pages. The Design tab sets

  • the type and where the inlet stands;
  • the stations that give its size and shape;
  • the ramp and cover;
  • the diverter, or the lack of one;
  • the number and roll angles of the inlets.

Quick Start

  1. Add the body, an MDC_BOD, and an MDC_INLET as its child. A new MDC_INLET is a top-mounted two-dimensional inlet, 3 long, at X = 4, on the default MDC_BOD.
  2. Choose INTYPE, and set XINLT to the inlet's leading edge. The inlet stands on the body there.
  3. Set the five stations' positions X, heights H and widths W.
  4. Choose whether the inlet is open or covered with COVER, and give an open inlet its RAMP angle.
  5. Set the diverter's HDIV, XDIV and LDIV, or set HDIV to zero for no diverter.
  6. Set NIN, the number of inlets, and, if they are not evenly spaced, turn on PHI given and give each its roll angle.

Types of Inlet

INTYPE is one of three types (Figure 1).

  • 2DTOP: a top-mounted two-dimensional inlet, a box whose height H is out from the body and whose width W is across it.
  • 2DSIDE: a side-mounted two-dimensional inlet, the same box turned so that its height is across the body and its width out from it. The heights of the stations share one side, so the inlet is flush with that side where it is shorter than the tallest.
  • AXI: an axisymmetric inlet, a body of revolution whose diameters are the widths W, with its axis straight, above the body. Its heights are not used.

Figure 1. The three types, each with the default stations, except that the axisymmetric inlet comes to a point at its leading edge, W(1) of zero. The side-mounted inlet is rolled to the side of the body, PHI 270.

All three are solids: the ramp, the cowl and the body behind it, and a diverter where there is one.

Placing an Inlet

An inlet stands on the mold line, the surface of the body. DATCOM takes the body's radius there from the body, and so does an MDC_INLET that is a child of an MDC_BOD: each inlet stands at the distance from the body's axis to its mold line under the inlet's leading edge, in the direction of its own roll angle, so a body that is elliptical or cambered is followed. The inlet keeps that radius along its length. RBODY then shows the first inlet's radius and is greyed. Any other MDC_INLET takes the mold line to be a cylinder of radius RBODY about the X axis; RBODY is not an input of DATCOM's namelist.

XINLT is the station of the inlet's leading edge, along XX from the origin, as DATCOM measures it. It is the X location of the XForm tab, shown again on the Design tab.

The inner face of the inlet is HDIV out from the mold line: the plane of H=0H = 0 for a two-dimensional inlet, and the lowest line of the cowl for an axisymmetric one, whose axis is half of W(3) above it. The heights H are measured from the inner face.

Stations

The inlet's shape is given at five stations along it (Figure 2), and runs straight between them: the leading edge or tip, the cowl lip, the start of the midbody, the start of the boattail and the end of the boattail. The position of station ii along the inlet is X(i), measured from the leading edge, which is at X=0X = 0, so X(1) is not an input. The height H(i) and the width W(i) are the section's size there; the leading edge has no height, so H(1) is not an input either, and an axisymmetric inlet has no H at all. An axisymmetric inlet's W(i) is its diameter, and its W(1) is its tip, a point where it is zero. The Design tab has a block of inputs for each station.

Figure 2. The five stations of a top-mounted two-dimensional inlet, from the side and from above.

The inlet is closed at its aft end, after station 5, by a flat face. DATCOM requires the inlet to be boattailed, its section at station 5 smaller than at station 4; that is not required here. A station that is not aft of the one before it is moved to just aft of it.

Ramp and Cover

An open inlet has a ramp in front of the cowl lip: a wedge, or for an axisymmetric inlet a cone, from the leading edge to station 2, at the angle RAMP above the inner face (Figure 3). Across the lip, a flat face runs from the end of the ramp up to the cowl, where the flow enters. A covered inlet, with COVER on, is plugged flush with the inlet face: a plane, or for an axisymmetric inlet a cone, from the leading edge to the cowl lip, with no ramp and no opening. RAMP is then greyed. A ramp steeper than that plane or cone would reach the cowl before the lip, and is the plug as well. An axisymmetric inlet whose tip is wide, as the default is, is this way: its ramp is the plug unless W(1) is small.

Figure 3. An inlet with a ramp of 25 degrees, and the same inlet covered.

Diverter

HDIV is the height of the inner face above the mold line, and sets how the inlet meets the body (Figure 4).

  • With HDIV above zero, the inlet stands off the body, and a diverter fills the gap between them.
  • With HDIV zero, the inlet is conformal. It is sunk a hundredth of RBODY into the body, so that it crosses the body rather than touching it along a line. There is no diverter.
  • With HDIV below zero, the inlet is semi-submerged, its inner face that far below the mold line. There is no diverter.

Figure 4. Sections of an inlet in its midbody, with a diverter, conformal, and semi-submerged.

A diverter is a plate, standing from just below the mold line up to halfway up the inlet. It begins XDIV behind the leading edge, as a point, and its width grows linearly to its aft end, LDIV further, where it is 95 percent of the inlet's width across the body. It is nowhere wider than that, so it is a little narrower than the inlet, and it ends no further aft than the inlet does (Figure 5). Its XDIV and LDIV are greyed where there is no diverter.

Figure 5. The diverter of the inlet of Figure 2, from the side and from above.

Locations

NIN is the number of inlets in the set, from 1 to 20, all the same. They are rolled around the body at the station XINLT, each at its angle PHI. An angle is measured clockwise from the top looking forward, so 90 degrees is to the right, as the roll angles of the MDC_PROTUB and MDC_FIN components are (Figure 6). Unless PHI given is on, the inlets are evenly spaced around the body, the first at the top, and the angles are set from NIN; the PHI slider shows them but is greyed. With PHI given on, each angle is set by hand. The Inlet selector chooses which one the slider shows. An angle that has not been set is zero. INTYPE only orients the inlet; the angle puts it on the body, so a side-mounted inlet is at a PHI of 90 or 270 degrees, and a top-mounted one at 0.

Figure 6. Three axisymmetric inlets of a set evenly spaced, from behind the body.

Parameters

The parms are in the Design group but for XINLT, which is the XForm tab's X location. Each station has a block of inputs in the Design tab, the position, height and width of the station. There is a PHI for each of the NIN inlets, made and deleted as NIN changes, at the next update.
